## 1. San Francisco & San José Railroad, the Bayshore Cutoff, and San José in 1935

### Operator and incorporation

- SF&SJ, then Southern Pacific. Caltrain [O]: in 1870 the "San Francisco and San Jose Railroad is
  absorbed into Southern Pacific."
- Incorporated "July 21st, 1860": *San Francisco Business Directory and Mercantile Guide for
  1864–65*, p. 283 [P][img], and Langley's 1867 directory, p. 37 [P][img]. Wikipedia gives Aug 18,
  1860.

### Openings

| Stretch | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The Mission to Big Tree Station on San Francisquito Creek | 1863-10-17 | Directory 1864–65, p. 283 [P][img]: "On the 17th day of October, 1863, the first train passed over the portion of the road finished, from the Mission to Big Tree Station on the San Francisquito Creek." |
| San Francisco to Mayfield, regular service | 1863-10 | Caltrain [O] |
| To San José | 1864-01-16 | Directory, p. 283 [P][img]: "On the 16th day of January, 1864, the road was completed to San Jose"; Caltrain [O] |
| San Francisco end to Fourth & Brannan | 1864-02-14 | Directory, p. 283 [P][img] |

### The first San Francisco end

- **First terminal.** Caltrain [O]: "The San Francisco terminal initially is located at 18th and
  Valencia streets."
- **1864:** a depot on Brannan St between Third and Fourth (Directory 1864–65, p. 282 [P][img]).
- **By 1867, Market & Valencia.** Langley 1867, p. 37 [P][img]: "The principal depot at present is
  at the corner of Valencia and Market Streets."
- **By 1876, Fourth & Townsend.** Langley 1876 [P]: "passenger depot, NE cor Fourth and Townsend".
- **The route out of the city:**
  - The 1899 USGS San Francisco sheet [O][img] draws the line ("S. P. R. R.", "Monterey line")
    south from Mission Bay, then diagonally southwest.
  - Wikipedia [W] names tracks on Harrison St and the Bernal Cut (March 1863).
  - The 1864 Britton & Co. railroad map of San Francisco (David Rumsey 4779.000, CC BY-NC-SA) is
    the primary map for 1864 (decision 7).

### Bayshore Cutoff

- **Opened 1907-12-08.**
  - SF Call, Nov 30, 1907, p. 5, an advertisement [P][ocr]: "Bay Shore Cutoff to be formally opened
    and in operation December 8th".
  - SF Call, Dec 8, 1907, p. 24 [P][ocr].
- **North end.** *Railroad Gazette* 42(11), Mar 15, 1907, pp. 328–331 [P][ocr]: "from existing
  tracks of the Southern Pacific in Seventh street"; "to junction with the original line at San
  Bruno, is 9.84 miles."
- **The junction, at San Bruno.**
  - SF Call, Dec 8, 1907: "the junction with the old line at San Bruno".
  - USGS Bulletin 614 (1915), p. 130 [O]: "At San Bruno the railroad branches."
  - On the USGS San Mateo 15′ sheet of 1915 (scan 298922) [O][img], the cutoff merges into the old
    line a few hundred feet north of San Bruno station: about 37.630 N, 122.414 W (read off the
    sheet, within about 50 m).
- **The old line after 1907 (the Ocean View line).**
  - Passengers were suspended in 1928 for highway work (CRC Decision 20459).
  - CRC Decision 22212 (Mar 18, 1930) [O] let SP "abandon its passenger trains Nos. 130 and 131
    operating upon its so-called Ocean View line between San Francisco and San Bruno".
  - The track's later abandonments (1942 and after) rest on Wikipedia [W] only.

### San José, 1935

- **The old line.** CRC Decision 3351 (1916) [O] says the main line "enters the City from the
  northwest near the end of Autumn Street, runs eastward across San Pedro, North First, North Second
  and North Third streets and then turns southerly and follows Fourth Street … to Reed Street".
- **The new line.** *Southern Pacific Bulletin*, January 1936, p. 7 [P][img]:
  - "The new line was opened to traffic at 12:01 a.m., December 31."
  - "All traffic has ceased over the old line."
  - It "leaves the old line at Polhemus Street … along the route of the Santa Cruz Branch … to the
    new station built on the site of the old South Pacific Coast depot", then "to a connection with
    the main line at Lick".
  - Caltrain [O]: December 1935, "A new terminal opens on Cahill Street".
- **Conflict:** the length of the relocation is 4.4, about 5 or 4.5 miles, depending on the
  source.

### Today's network on it

- **Caltrain:**
  - San Francisco to San Bruno is the 1907 cutoff.
  - San Bruno to Santa Clara is the 1863–64 corridor (later local realignments not checked).
  - Through San José it is the 1935 line from Polhemus St through Cahill St.
- **BART and I-280** reuse the Ocean View right-of-way from Glen Park to Daly City [W].

## 2. The original Western Pacific (1862), Central Pacific, SF & Oakland and SF & Alameda

Sources: Halley, *The Centennial Year Book of Alameda County* (1876) [C], and *History of Alameda
County* (Wood, 1883) [C].

- **San Francisco & Oakland Railroad:**
  - "The first trip … was made on the 2d day of September, 1863", from the end of the wharf to
    Broadway (Halley p. 177).
  - Extended to Larue's Wharf, Brooklyn, 1865-04-01 (Halley p. 200).
  - Ran along Seventh St: the corridor of the SP Seventh Street line already drawn.
  - The wharf was "completed, in January, 1871"; the Oakland Mole was begun June 1879 and "in 1881
    was completed" (1883 history, pp. 629–631).
- **San Francisco & Alameda Railroad** (Halley):
  - Trial trip 1864-08-13; "as far as High Street" 1864-08-25 (p. 190).
  - To San Leandro 1865-03-01 (pp. 197–198).
  - To Haywards 1865-08-25 (p. 198). Halley's "25th of August" for both years needs a page check.
  - The Alameda ferry was abandoned 1873-09-29 (1883 history, p. 630).
- **Western Pacific (1862):**
  - San José to Niles Canyon, 1866 (NPS [O]).
  - First through train from Sacramento at San Leandro, Monday 1869-09-06 (Halley p. 279).
  - "The last rail of the Pacific Railroad was laid on the 29th of October, in Oakland"; first
    passenger through train from Oakland Point 1869-11-08 (Halley pp. 280–281).
  - Through Niles Canyon until 1984 (NPS [O]). The NPS page's "early 1870" completion contradicts
    Halley; Halley is taken.
- **Central Pacific north of Oakland:**
  - Oakland Point to the Willows 1876-07-27 (Halley p. 435).
  - Through Antioch 1878-09-09 (*History of Contra Costa County*, 1882 [C]).

## 3. Other railroads

- **North Pacific Coast** (narrow gauge):
  - "first opened January, 1875" (*History of Marin County*, 1880, pp. 147–148 [C]).
  - To Duncan's Mills: "the winter of 1876-7" (Marin history) vs "May 15, 1877" (*Poor's Manual*,
    1900).
  - The San Rafael & San Quentin's first trip was 1870-03-21 (Marin history, p. 333).
- **San Francisco & North Pacific:**
  - Petaluma–Santa Rosa, October 1870 (*History of Sonoma County*, 1880, pp. 301–302 [C]).
  - Cloverdale in 1872. Tiburon's opening is unsourced.
- **Napa Valley Railroad:** Soscol to Napa 1865-07-11; Calistoga October 1868 (*History of Napa and
  Lake Counties*, 1881 [C]).
- **California Pacific:** Vallejo–Suisun 1868-06-24 and more, Wikipedia only [W].
- **Santa Fe at Point Richmond:** first overland passenger service 1900-07-01 (SF Call, Jul 2, 1900,
  p. 3 [P][ocr]). Its own passenger ferries ended in 1933 [W].
- **South Pacific Coast:**
  - Alameda–Los Gatos regular service 1878-06-01 (1883 history, p. 833).
  - Oakland branch "Alameda Jc. to 14th St., Oakland 1.8" miles (*Poor's Manual*, 1900).
  - Leased to SP from 1887-07-01.
- **California & Nevada:** pointers only, and they conflict.
- **Oakland, Antioch & Eastern:**
  - Openings are pointers only (1911, 1913).
  - Passenger service ended under CRC Decisions 33416 (1940) and 34232 (1941) [O].
- **SP San Ramon branch:** first regular trip 1891-06-07, Avon to San Ramon; on to Radum in 1909
  (City of San Ramon [O]).
- **SP Dumbarton Cutoff:**
  - Niles via Newark to Redwood City, 16 miles (*Railroad Gazette*, 1907 [P][ocr]).
  - The bridge opened 1910-09-24, freight only (SF Call, Sep 24, 1910, p. 10 [P][ocr]).

## Conflicts

1. **SF&SJ first train:** Oct 17, 1863 to Big Tree Station (directories) vs Caltrain's "October 1863
   … Mayfield"; Wikipedia gives Oct 17 to Menlo Park and Oct 23.
2. **SF&SJ incorporation:** Jul 21 vs Aug 18, 1860.
3. **First San Francisco terminal:** 18th & Valencia (Caltrain) vs "the Mission" (directories).
4. **The 1869 completion:** Halley's Sep 6 and Nov 8, 1869 vs NPS's "early 1870".
5. **North Pacific Coast to Duncan's Mills:** winter 1876–77 vs May 15, 1877.
6. **The Vallejo ferry's start:** 1895 vs 1896.
7. **Santa Fe at Point Richmond:** Jul 1, 1900 (SF Call) vs Jul 3 or 4 (secondary sources).
8. **The San José relocation's length:** 4.4, about 5, or 4.5 miles.
