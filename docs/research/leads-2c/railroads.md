### Route in words, and stations

- **Western Pacific, as planned and built** (Wikipedia [W]): "San Jose north to Niles, then east
  through the canyon [Alameda Cañon, now Niles Canyon], then through Pleasanton, Livermore,
  Altamont Pass, and Stockton before reaching Sacramento"; 123 miles San José–Sacramento. Niles
  was Vallejo's Mills, renamed for Addison Niles [W]. The canyon is that of Alameda Creek
  (Museum of Local History [W]: the 1866 track "reached into Alameda Creek canyon").
- **Into Oakland:**
  - **September–November 1869:** from San Leandro over the SF&A to the Alameda wharf (Halley [r];
    CHL 440).
  - **From 1869-11-08:** to Oakland Point along Seventh St, the SF&O line (HABS [x]).
  - **How trains got from San Leandro to Seventh St before 1870-05-15** (the San Leandro–Melrose
    opening [W]) is not sourced. The candidates are the San Francisco Bay Railroad's 22.5 miles
    (consolidated November 1869) or a connection off the SF&A. This needs Wood (1883), Halley
    pp. 279–300, or the *Alta* of Nov 9, 1869.
- **North shore:** Oakland – Shell Mound (Emeryville) – the Willows (?) – Martinez – Antioch –
  Tracy. The intermediate places (Pinole, Rodeo, Vallejo Junction, Port Costa) are **not sourced**
  here.
- **Station snapshot:** none sourced. Best candidates:
  - Halley's 1876 gazetteer (towns along the WP);
  - the Board of Railroad Commissioners' report for 1880 (station lists and distances per road);
  - an 1880s SP timetable.

### Route in words, and stations

- **SF&O, 1863** (LocalWiki Oakland [W][x]): "from Oakland's Point wharf, along 7th St. to Broadway
  station", with the ferry to San Francisco's Davis Street wharf. In 1865 it went on to Brooklyn
  (Larue's Wharf) [r]. This is the corridor of the later SP Seventh Street line already researched
  (`sp-east-bay.md`).
- **SF&A, 1864–65:**
  - **The wharf** (Wikipedia [W][x]): "3,750-foot-long … wharf, starting from a spot near today's
    Pacific Avenue and Main Street westward".
  - **Through Alameda** (Alameda Post [W][x]): "Alameda Station situated on Railroad Avenue
    (today's Lincoln Avenue) at Park Street". Wikipedia [W]: Linnet St, later Railroad Ave, now
    Lincoln Ave. An Encinal station stood east of Grand St [W].
  - **Railroad Ave was renamed Lincoln Ave in 1909** (Alameda Museum Quarterly, Sept 2023 [W][x]:
    "Lincoln was called Railroad Avenue until 1909").
  - **Beyond Alameda:** High St (5 miles from the wharf [W]), then the crossing to the mainland,
    then San Leandro and Haywards. **The crossing and the streets in San Leandro and Haywards are
    not sourced.**
- **Station snapshot:** none sourced for 1865 or 1869.

### Today's track vs gone

- **All gone as steam railroads.**
  - Seventh St carried the SP electric line to 1941 (`sp-east-bay.md`).
  - Lincoln Ave carried the SP's electric Lincoln Avenue line to 1941 (same file); the rails were
    removed in 1960 [W].
- **Seventh St in West Oakland:** whether any of today's track uses the alignment isn't sourced.
  BART runs above 7th St, but that's a different structure.
