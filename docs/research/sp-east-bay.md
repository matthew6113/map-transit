# Research notes: the SP East Bay electric lines (Red Trains) and the IER, 1911–1941

Gathered Oct 14, 2026 for the Phase 2a pilot. These notes are the trail behind `data/past/sp-east-bay/`;
every fact in the data cites its source in `data/sources.json`, with pages.

## How it was sourced

- **California Railroad Commission (CRC) decisions**, 19 of them, read as PDFs from
  `files.cpuc.ca.gov` (reached through the web tools; the container's own requests are blocked).
  Quotes marked [img] were checked against the page image; page numbers are the typed ones.
- **Railway Age Gazette** (RAG) and **Electric Railway Journal** (ERJ): archive.org's OCR text.
- **San Francisco Call**: Chronicling America (loc.gov). These quotes keep raw OCR errors and should
  be re-read on the page image before they are quoted in the map.
- Not usable from here: Google Books text, HathiTrust, CDNC, loc.gov OCR pages, the RAG PDF.

## System-wide

1. **The 1909–12 project** (RAG, Sep 13, 1912, pp. 460–463, Guppy):
   - "the old steam lines electrified, a total of 29.3 miles of road"; "21.1 miles of new
     extensions"; "Of the old steam lines, 19.6 miles are on private right-of-way and 9.7 miles on
     public streets, and of the extensions 4.3 miles are on private right of way and 16.8 miles on
     streets."
   - "Work was begun in the spring of 1909."
   - Two steam systems before: one "radiating from Alameda Mole" (Alameda, and Oakland by the
     Webster Street line); the other from Oakland Pier to "Oakland, East Oakland, Fruitdale [sic] and
     Melrose toward the east and the city of Berkeley and the town of Emeryville toward the north."
   - Overnight yards "at the easterly end of Oakland, the northerly end of Berkeley, the easterly
     end of Alameda and at Fourteenth and Franklin streets."
2. **Lines in 1936** (CRC D28671, Mar 23, 1936, p. 5): "From Oakland Pier there radiate three
   electric rail lines … SEVENTH STREET LINE, BERKELEY LINE, and NINTH STREET LINE. From Alameda Pier
   … the ENCINAL AVENUE LINE … and the LINCOLN AVENUE LINE"; plus one peak-hour train "between
   Alameda Pier and Dutton Avenue (North San Leandro) via City of Alameda and Fruitvale Bridge."
3. **Lines in 1939–40** (CRC D33445, Aug 24, 1940, p. 7 [img], IER Exhibit 25), route miles:

   | Line | Route mi | Bridge | Private ROW | Paved street | Not joint |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Ninth Street | 12.98 | 7.18 | 2.40 | 3.40 | 4.91 |
   | Shattuck Avenue | 12.70 | 7.18 | 2.70 | 2.82 | 5.52 |
   | Seventh Street | 19.03 | 7.19 | 6.40 | 5.44 | 12.14 |
   | Encinal Avenue | 19.76 | 7.19 | 5.60 | 6.97 | 6.50 |
   | Lincoln Avenue | 18.02 | 7.19 | 5.60 | 5.23 | 3.46 |

   SP freight used IER track "on the Seventh Street line between Havenscourt and Dowling, and between
   Fallon Street and Melrose; on the Alameda Lincoln Avenue line between its point of junction with
   the Seventh Street line and West Alameda; on the Shattuck Avenue line from near the 34th Street
   junction to Dwight Way; and on the Ninth Street line from its junction with the Shattuck Avenue
   line to a point near Heinz Avenue."
4. **Bay Bridge era** (CRC D33445):
   - p. 9 [img]: "It was not until January 15, 1939, that the bridge was opened to rail service."
   - p. 9 [img]: "Except for bridge connecting tracks the rail lines serving the East Bay area were
     unchanged when ferry boat operation was discontinued."
   - p. 3 [img]: jointly used with Key System, "the service yards and facilities on the eastern
     approach to the bridge, including the overhead wye at 26th Street in Oakland. From the East Bay
     yards the lines of the two companies diverge."
   - p. 8: "repair, storage, and switching facilities were provided on the Key System Mole near the
     eastern approach to the bridge" (state-owned, California Toll Bridge Authority).
   - p. 6: track "west of the overhead wye is owned by the State of California … the remainder east
     of the wye is owned by Southern Pacific Company … and leased to Interurban."
5. **Bridge connecting tracks** (1937 CRC orders): D29615 (Mar 22, 1937), the bridge railway's
   "northerly leg" and "south leg" over SP tracks "in the vicinity of 26th Street", and a
   "relocation of existing railway tracks in Wood Street, north of 34th Street"; D30015 (Aug 9, 1937),
   SP "Off-Line" tracks crossing "Eighth Street, Shorey Street, Bay and Goss Streets …, a portion of
   7th Street, in the City of Oakland, and … a portion of Fernside Boulevard in the City of Alameda";
   D29614 (Mar 22, 1937), grade separation over Key System tracks "on which westbound Southern Pacific
   trains (Interurban Electric Railway Company) will operate"; D34389 p. 5 [img], the Berkeley lines
   ran "through the East Bay Yards, and over the 34th Street wye … Between the wye and Ninth Street
   junction operations are over private right of way."
6. **The IER**: D28671 (Mar 23, 1936) let it take trackage rights over all SP electric lines in
   Alameda County and run over the bridge once open. SP and IER filed Application 19704 on
   "November 16, 1934" (p. 14). When the IER actually began operating is **not sourced**; D33445 p. 9
   calls SP "Interurban's predecessor" in the ferry era.

## Line by line

### Seventh Street line (in 1941 also "Dutton Avenue Line")

- Route (CRC D17743, Dec 11, 1926, p. 2 [img]): "from Oakland Mole via Seventh Street, private
  right-of-way, Bond Street, Beck Street, Blanche Street and Bancroft Avenue to Dutton Avenue in San
  Leandro. The route from Bond Street to Bancroft Avenue is interspersed with short lengths of
  private rights-of-way." Local service "on Seventh Street from Pine Street as far as Fallon
  Street," and "its right-of-way from Fallon Street to Melrose Station" (p. 7). RAG p. 463: SP had to
  "pave the entire width of the street … for a distance of approximately 2½ miles."
- D34060 (Apr 1, 1941) p. 3 [img]: the bus ran "106th Avenue, and Bancroft Avenue to Estudillo
  Avenue in San Leandro, about one-half mile beyond the former rail terminus. From 106th Avenue to
  Dutton Avenue, the motor coach line follows the same route as the former rail line." The line
  crossed East 14th Street between Melrose and Havenscourt (order IV). Crossings (D34053, Mar 25,
  1941): Tenth Street (G-10.65), 12th Street (G-10.76), 73rd Avenue (G-12.93).
- Stations: Melrose and Havenscourt, "only 1.74 miles" apart (D34060 p. 6); Dowling (p. 7); 82nd
  Avenue (implied, p. 7); Seminary Avenue (D17743 p. 4).
- Dates: last steam on Seventh Street the night of Dec 30, 1911 (SF Call, Dec 31, 1911, p. 22: "For the
  last time tonight the old familiar locomotive whistle sounded in Seventh street"); the first
  electric day isn't stated. Dutton Avenue extension: unsourced (Wikipedia points to the Oakland
  Tribune, Jul 27, 1912, p. 4). IER would not run "beyond March 1st, 1941" (D33891, Feb 11, 1941, p. 5);
  Key System's substitute service began "on March 22, 1941" (D34060 pp. 2, 4).

### Eighteenth Street line (Oakland)

- c. 1921–26 (D17743 p. 2 [img]): "from Oakland Mole to 14th and Franklin Street via Sixteenth Street
  Station, 18th Street, 20th Street and Franklin Street. Westbound … over 21st Street and Jones Street
  instead of over Twentieth Street." Moved from Franklin to Webster Street in 1926 (D14618, D16083,
  D17028). Final description (D25740, Mar 16, 1933, p. 10): "along Eighteenth, Twentieth,
  Twenty-first and Webster Streets, between Sixteenth Street Station and Second and Webster Streets,"
  2.9 miles (p. 4).
- Abandoned "On the 26th day of March, 1933" (D27800, Mar 4, 1935, p. 4 [img]). Opening: unsourced.

### Alameda lines

- **Webster Street line** (D17743 p. 2 [img]): "from 14th and Franklin Street to Alameda Pier via
  Webster Street and the Harrison Street Swing Bridge." Electric in mid-1911 (SF Call, Jul 1, 1911,
  p. 14); exact start unsourced. Ended: SP "may cease the operation … on or after December 27th,
  1923" (D12906, Dec 7, 1923); "discontinued on December 27th, 1923" (D17743 p. 2).
- **Encinal Avenue and Lincoln Avenue lines** (D33732, Dec 17, 1940, pp. 7–8 [img]): in 1939–41
  routed over the bridge, the Seventh Street line to Fruitvale Junction, the Fruitvale Bridge, then
  diverging over "generally … parallel tracks throughout the entire residential portion of the
  city," rejoining "at the western end of the island" to a terminus at West Alameda; before 1939 they
  ran to the Alameda Mole. Streets east of Webster Street, from the bus plan that followed "the
  same streets now traversed by the rail lines east of Webster Street, except for necessary
  deviations at terminal loops" (p. 9): Lincoln Avenue; Encinal Avenue and Central Avenue. Both
  "electrified in 1911" (p. 8). First electric service June 1, 1911 (ERJ, Jun 17, 1911, p. 1048: "on
  June 1 the Alameda line was opened"; SF Call, Jun 3, 1911, p. 18). Key System buses "placed in effect
  January 18, 1941" (D33891 p. 4 [img]).
- **Alameda Mole** (RAG p. 461): "four parallel main tracks from the terminal yard at the ferry slips
  to West Alameda, about two miles." West Alameda shops "at the junction of the Oakland and Alameda
  lines running to the Alameda Mole" (ERJ, Jun 17, 1911, p. 1052).

### Shattuck Avenue (Berkeley) line

- Route (D34389, Jul 3, 1941, p. 5 [img]): from Ninth Street junction "principally over open track in
  private right of way along Stanford Avenue and Adeline Street, through a portion of Emeryville and
  North Oakland to Berkeley, … over private right of way along Adeline Street to Shattuck Avenue, …
  along Shattuck Avenue in paved-in right of way to Bancroft Avenue, … in paved street along Shattuck
  Avenue to a point between Vine and Rose Street, … over private right of way and paved street to a
  terminus at Thousand Oaks Station at Colusa Street and Solano Avenue."
- Northbrae station at "Sutter and Hopkins Streets … about one-half mile short of the former
  terminus" (D34460, Aug 1, 1941, p. 4 [img]). The North Berkeley tunnel (RAG p. 462): "465 ft. long
  …, where the line passes under a point from which several streets radiate."
- Trial trip Dec 22, 1911 (SF Call, Dec 23, 1911, p. 17). Key System's substitute service began July
  26, 1941 (D34460 p. 1).

### California Street and Ellsworth Street lines (D25740, Mar 16, 1933, p. 5 [img])

- California: "over the track of the Shattuck Avenue Line to California Junction, thence … 3.5 miles
  … northerly along California Street, Monterey Avenue and Colusa Avenue to … Thousand Oaks Station."
- Ellsworth: "to Adeline and Woolsey Streets, thence … 1.5 miles … easterly along Woolsey Street and
  private rights of way to Ellsworth Street, thence northerly along Ellsworth Street to … Bancroft
  Way."
- Abandonment authorized Mar 16, 1933; the dates of opening and of the last trains are unsourced
  here.

### Ninth Street line

- Route (D34460 p. 3 [img]): "from Thousand Oaks station along Solano Avenue across San Pablo Avenue,
  thence south over private right of way to Harrison Street, thence in paved street along Ninth
  Street through West Berkeley to Heinz Avenue, thence generally over private right of way through
  Emeryville to the … Bay Bridge." Ended with the Shattuck line (substitute from July 26, 1941).
  Opening: Wikipedia gives Feb 7, 1912 (Berkeley Gazette, Feb 8, 1912), not yet checked.

## Conflicts

1. The Webster Street line crossed the estuary by the Harrison Street bridge (CRC), not the Webster
   Street bridge.
2. Its last day: CRC says service could end "on or after December 27th, 1923" and was "discontinued
   on December 27th"; Wikipedia's Oakland Tribune of Dec 26 says "S.P. Bridge Service to End Tonight".
3. The Eighteenth Street line ended Mar 26, 1933 (CRC D27800), not Mar 31.
4. D25740 is dated Mar 16, 1933 on its signed page; D27800 says Mar 15.
5. Whether electric service on Seventh Street began Dec 30 or 31, 1911.
6. Bridge rail service opened Jan 15, 1939 (CRC); Wikipedia's IER history says January 1938.
7. When the IER began operating: 1934, 1938 or January 1939 in secondary sources; unsourced here.
