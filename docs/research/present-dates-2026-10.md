# Today's network: the undated lines, Oct 28, 2026

A pass over the present-era lines that never appear on the year slider. One is now dated: the Angel
Island–Tiburon ferry, 1959 (Town of Tiburon, Resolution No. 36-2024). For the rest, this note records
what was found and why each stays undated, so the next pass starts here.

`vta.org` is reachable from the build container now. `sfbayferry.com`, `goldengate.org`,
`alamedaca.gov`, `mtc.ca.gov`, `townoftiburon.org`, `parks.ca.gov`, `cpuc.ca.gov`, `amtrak.com`,
`winetrain.com`, `ncry.org` and `wrm.org` are not; facts from them come from search results only.

## VTA light rail (Blue, Green and part of the Orange line)

- **Found:** "The entire 21-mile light rail line would open for service April 21, 1991" (VTA's
  timeline, read on vta.org). VTA's pages say its light rail began in 1987; one search summary gave
  December 11, 1987 from a VTA page and another found no day, so the day is unconfirmed. VTA's 2000
  Measure A compliance report for FY2023 (read on vta.org, PDF p. 18):
  "The 5.3-mile Vasona Light Rail line (Phase 1) between Downtown San Jose and Campbell was opened in
  October 2005."
- **Missing:** where each stage of the Guadalupe line began and ended (1987, 1988, 1990, 1991), and
  where Tasman West begins. No official source found names them. Wikipedia's station articles give
  June 17, 1988 downtown and April 25, 1991 to Santa Teresa (their sources weren't checked), and the
  1991 day conflicts with VTA's April 21.
- **A question for Matthew:** for much of the way to Campbell the Vasona line runs along the
  Southern Pacific's old line, which carried passengers long before 2005 (`sp-peninsula`, the
  Vasona Branch trains). A segment's `opened` is when its track first carried passengers, so dating
  the stretch 2005 would contradict the past era. Either the light rail's own track counts as a new
  segment (dated 2005), or the corridor takes the Southern Pacific's date, which is a past fact.

## Ferries

- **Oakland & Alameda:** SF Bay Ferry's history puts it under 1989: "The City of Alameda eventually
  started the Alameda Oakland Ferry Service". "Eventually" gives no year, and MTC's history says only
  that after the Loma Prieta earthquake MTC "shepherds the launch of new ferry service between
  Oakland, Alameda and San Francisco". No day or year found in an official source.
- **Harbor Bay:** no start found. The city ran it, by contract with Harbor Bay Maritime, until it
  passed to SF Bay Ferry on July 1, 2012.
- **Golden Gate's Triangle:** GTFS draws it between San Francisco, Angel Island and Tiburon. Golden
  Gate began Angel Island service on December 13, 2021 (its timeline). Its documents, as search shows
  them, date weekend Tiburon service, which shares the Angel Island boat, three ways: January 2022
  (the FY2022 financial statements), January 31, 2022 (the FY2022-23 budget) and February 5, 2022
  (a 2022 general manager's report). None calls it the Triangle, so it stays undated until a document
  ties the name to a date.

## Heritage railways

The operators' own sites give dates: the Napa Valley Wine Train's first trip on September 16, 1989
(winetrain.com) and the Niles Canyon Railway's first train on May 21, 1988 (ncry.org). Both are
private operators, not public agencies, so under today's rule (official sources only) both stay null.
**A question for Matthew:** may an operator's own statement date its own service? The track under
both is older and is a past fact, which may come from any citable source.

The Western Railway Museum: no start date or current schedule found.

## The Coast Starlight

Amtrak's pages, as search shows them, don't give the train's first run. Three stretches of its track
are older Southern Pacific line, a past fact (`docs/checks/present.md`).
