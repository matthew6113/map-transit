# Research: Petaluma River passenger steamers and the Vallejo – Mare Island ferry

Gathered Oct 16, 2026, for the past era. Used for `psr-steamers` and `mare-island-ferry` in
`data/past/ferries/`. Builds on `docs/research/leads-2c/ferries-northbay.md` §3d–3e and
`docs/research/sources-2c/ferries-northbay.md` §3d.

## How this was gathered

- archive.org worked this session (us.archive.org hosts only). Page images were fetched through
  BookReader (`BookReaderImages.php`, by leaf or jp2 file) and read on screen. CPUC legacy decision PDFs
  were fetched from `files.cpuc.ca.gov` and rendered with `pdftoppm`.
- To look for an official end of steamer passengers, every 1935, 1936 and 1937 CRC decision PDF and
  every 1950 PUC decision PDF in the CPUC legacy folders was downloaded, text-extracted (first 2–3 pages,
  the caption) and grepped for "Petaluma" and "Mare Island"; Sept–Dec 1935 were also searched in full
  text. That turned up the Case 3824 follow-up D29778 (1937), which dates the passenger end. The bulk
  PDFs were then deleted.
- **Headline:** Petaluma passenger steamers ended **1935-12-23** [O][img]; the Mare Island ferry ran
  until **1986-08-29** (newsletter report [img]), not "after WWII".
- The pass's downloads were not kept; each entry's archive.org id or CPUC file reopens it.

**Tags** (as in the earlier research)

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [P] | Primary: a newspaper, timetable, directory, trade journal or company publication of the day |
| [O] | Official: CRC/CPUC decision, Board of State Harbor Commissioners, Federal Register |
| [C] | Contemporary compiled history (county histories 1880, 1889, 1911) |
| [S] | Later secondary history with footnotes (railfan journal, historical-society journal) |
| [W] | Pointer only (Wikipedia, blog, hobby newsletter memory, snippet of a lending-library book) |

### 1d. Conflicts (Petaluma)

- **Passenger end: resolved.** CRC D29778 (1937-05-24) p. 16 [O][img]: discontinued and tariff
  cancelled **effective 1935-12-23**. This agrees with Borden's year (p. 26 [S][img]) and the 1952
  *Western Railroader* (p. 14 [ocr]: "Passenger service on the steamers ended in 1935"), and with
  D28283 [O][img] (decided 1935-10-14 on hearings of March–May 1935), which still describes passengers
  "on a regular schedule". The authorizing order itself was not found: no 1935 decision from September
  on mentions a Petaluma steamer in its full text (OCR is poor), so the authority may have been an
  informal tariff permission rather than a numbered decision. Use **1935-12-23** [O]; read it as the
  last day of service being on or just before that date.
- **Start of P&SR operation.** Borden: *Gold* bought December 1903 [S][img]; CRC: service "inaugurated in
  1904" [O][img]. The run itself predates both (the *Gold* from 1883 [C][img]); only the operator changed.
- **"Foot of Market Street" (SRJ 1905)** vs Jackson/Washington bulkhead (1903 and 1906 directories,
  1911 timetable). The trade journal is likely loose wording for the waterfront near the Ferry Building;
  prefer the directories and the company timetable.
- **Jackson vs Washington.** 1911 timetable "Jackson Street Bulkhead"; 1912 directory "Washington St
  Bulkhead"; 1896/1903/1906 "between Washington and Jackson". Same berth, probably; draw it on the
  bulkhead between the two streets.
- **The *Gold*'s end.** SP Bulletin 1947 p. 11 [img]: "The last of that name burned in 1928". Borden p. 26
  [img]: *Gold* (II) taken out of service 1935, dismantled 1940. Borden also says three boats were not
  named *Gold* successively (Gold I 1883–1920, Gold II ex-*Fort Bragg* 1921–1935). Prefer Borden (he cites
  P&SR records), but both are secondary.
- **Gold (I) fire day.** Borden p. 24 [ocr]: Nov 8, 1920; WR 1952 [ocr]: Nov 29, 1920. A *Sonoma Index
  Tribune* of 1920-11-13 (`casomvhs_002144`) reports the fire, so Nov 8 fits; not read on the page.
- **Schedule after 1915.** Borden p. 23 [img] "a round trip in two days"; p. 32 [img] "cut to one round
  trip although two boats were used" daily except Sunday. Internal inconsistency; not mapped.

### 2c. Conflicts (Mare Island)

- **End date.** Wikipedia ("discontinued after the end of World War II") is wrong for the passenger
  ferry: it ran in 1954 [O][img] and closed 1986-08-29 [W/P][img]. The 1986 date rests on a newsletter
  relaying a *West County Times* clipping; the CPUC legacy archive has no 1985 – Sept 1987 decisions
  online, so no official confirmation was possible.
- **Causeway vs ferry.** CRC D12159 (A8512, rehearing, 1923-05-31), vol. 23 p. 514 [img]: about half the
  yard's employees used the ferry, the rest the "government causeway" built "some years ago". The ferry
  and the causeway coexisted from c. 1919 to 1986; the causeway did not end the ferry.
  Quote: "Applicant has no direct competition in this service, but is subject to some extent to
  competition by reason of the government causeway" [O][img].
- **"Bought in 1921"** (*Fare Box*) vs CRC: Rodeo-Vallejo bought it 1922-03-21 and sold it April 1923
  [O][img]. Prefer the CRC. (The repo's [W] Medium blog, "Robert Rauhauge bought the *Vallejo* in 1923",
  fits April 1923 but is unsourced.)
- **Start.** 1854 (Farragut, *Fare Box* [W]); late 1850s row boat (EDV [W]); late 1860s steamer *Lizzie*
  (Lott [snip]). No primary source read.
