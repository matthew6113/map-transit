# East Bay streetcars, 1931–1948: routes and end dates from the CRC/CPUC decisions (session 2)

Gathered Oct 15, 2026 (research session 2). Builds on `docs/research/east-bay-electric.md`,
`key-system.md` and `docs/research/leads-2c/eastbay.md` (the plan), and replaces that plan's
`[srch]` items wherever the decision PDF was read here.

**How it was read.** Every decision below was downloaded with `curl` from
`https://files.cpuc.ca.gov/LegacyCPUCDecisionsAndResolutions/Decisions/<folder>/<file>.pdf` and
read with `pdftotext -layout` (the PDFs are scanned typescript with an OCR layer, Adobe Paper
Capture, 2010). Doubtful passages were checked on the page image (`pdftoppm -r 110`).

**Tags:** [O] official (CRC/CPUC decision); [P] primary (newspaper, journal); [W] pointer
(Wikipedia, railfan sites; never the sole source); [ocr] read from the OCR text only; [img] checked
on the page image. "p." is the decision's own typed page number (the "-1-", "-2-" at the foot),
which is also the PDF page unless noted. Quotes are verbatim, with obvious OCR errors fixed
(`[sic]` where the original itself is odd).

**Street names.** "Today" means the name in Overture roads 2026-09-23.1 (see the plan's [data]
checks). Renamings used below: East 14th St (Oakland) = International Blvd; Grove St = Martin
Luther King Jr Way; 22nd St = West Grand Ave; 20th St = Thomas L. Berkley Way. The renaming
sources are noted where found; otherwise "renaming unsourced here".

## 0. D40742 (Sept 23, 1947, A19502, 108th supplemental): not a rail list

- Read in full (4 pp.). **It is a motor coach order only**: it reroutes the No. 74 Ashby Avenue
  bus, extends it to Codornices Village in Albany, and splits the No. 67 Spruce Street bus. It
  does not list the 1947 streetcar network. [O][ocr]
- One rail-related fact: p. 2, the portion of the No. 65 bus route "between Southern Pacific
  Station along University Avenue to Sixth Street is now served by the No. 6 College Avenue Line"
  [O][ocr]. So in 1947 the "No. 6 College Avenue Line" (by then a bus; see line 6 below) ran on
  University Ave west to the SP station at Third St, Berkeley.

## 1. The March 1948 abandonment: lines 14, 15 and 18 (D41306)

**CPUC D41306, Mar 9, 1948, A19502 (110th supplemental).** Applicant Key System Transit Lines.
Read in full on the page images (14 pp.; the OCR layer is unusable). [O][img]

- **Line names** (order (1), p. 11): "street car service on its No. 14 - East 18th Street, No. 15 -
  14th Avenue-MacArthur Boulevard, and No. 18 - Park Boulevard and Lakeshore Avenue street rail
  lines in the City of Oakland". [O][img]
  - **Correction to the plan:** line 14 is **East 18th Street**, not "East 21st Street"
    (historyofpiedmont.com [W] was wrong), and line 15 is "14th Avenue-MacArthur Boulevard".
- **Hearing:** "before Commissioner Rowell and Examiner Paul in Oakland and San Francisco on
  February 11 and 16, 1948" (p. 2). [O][img]
- **Termini** (p. 2): "The No. 14 rail line begins at 13th and Jefferson Streets and terminates at
  Fruitvale Avenue and East 27th Street, Oakland. The No. 15 rail line also begins at 13th and
  Jefferson Streets and terminates at MacArthur Boulevard and 35th Avenue, Oakland." [O][img]
- **Line 18** (p. 2): "extends from Leimert Boulevard and Oakmore Road to downtown Oakland via Park
  Boulevard and other streets to 13th Street and Broadway, thence via Broadway, Grand Avenue,
  Lakeshore Boulevard and Walla Vista Avenue to a point near Carlston Avenue, thence a distance of
  about 626 feet beyond the latter point over a private right-of-way." [O][img]
  (The decision writes "Lakeshore Boulevard" here and "Lakeshore Avenue" in the footnote.)
- **The track given up, street by street** (p. 2, footnote 1, checked at 200 dpi): "The street car
  tracks, roadway and electrical overhead proposed to be abandoned are over the following
  streets:" [O][img]
  - "From Fruitvale Avenue and East 27th Street via East 27th Street, 26th Avenue, East 24th
    Street, 25th Avenue, East 21st Street, 14th Avenue and East 18th Street to private
    right-of-way near 3rd Avenue."
  - "Also from MacArthur Boulevard and 35th Avenue via MacArthur Boulevard and 14th Avenue to East
    21st Street."
  - "Also from Leimert Boulevard and Oakmore Road via Leimert Boulevard, Park Boulevard and private
    right-of-way to East 18th Street."
  - "Also from Lakeshore Avenue and Trestle Glen Road via Lakeshore Avenue, Walla Vista Avenue and
    private right-of-way to the vicinity of Portal and Ashmount Avenues."
  - The rest of each route was kept because it was "used for the operation of other rail lines
    which are not involved herein" (p. 2). [O][img]
- **Trackage already quitclaimed to Oakland** (p. 3): "By agreements, portions of the trackage used
  by the Nos. 14, 15 and 18 rail lines have been quitclaimed to the City of Oakland … (Dec. No.
  38438, Appl. No. 27097)". [O][img] → D38438 not yet read (see section 9).
- **Order:** (1) "may discontinue and abandon street car service" on the three lines (p. 11);
  (1)(a) poles and overhead of Nos. 15 and 18 to stay for one year (trolley-coach option asked by
  Oakland, pp. 9–10); (1)(c) "the abandonment of said street car service shall be made effective
  concurrently with the establishment of passenger stage service"; (5) bus service "not later than
  30 days after the effective date hereof"; "The effective date of this order shall be 10 days
  from the date hereof." Signed "this 9th day of March, 1948" (handwritten) (pp. 11, 14). [O][img]
  - **So rail service could end between Mar 19 and Apr 18, 1948.** The actual day isn't in the
    decision. (Plan's [W] pointer: Mar 28, 1948; see section 8 for the Tribune check.)
- **Side facts from the same decision** (useful for other lines) [O][img]:
  - p. 4: "the route of its present No. 2 rail line from San Pablo Avenue and Washington Street to
    7th and Pine Streets in Oakland"; "supplement the No. 2 rail line service on West 7th Street
    between Washington and Pine Streets by operation of its No. 5 rail line from its present
    terminus at 7th and Washington Streets via 7th Street to Pine Street."
  - p. 5: local service on East 14th St between downtown and 103rd Ave "is now provided by the No.
    1, East 14th Street car line".
  - Bus streets certificated (order (2) z, aa, p. 13) for the No. 72 bus over the No. 2's route:
    "On San Pablo Avenue and Washington Street between Ashby Avenue and 7th Street"; "On 7th
    Street between Broadway and Pine Streets."

## 2. The last four lines, Nov 1948: lines 1, 2, 4 and 5 (D42199)

**CPUC D42199, Nov 4, 1948, A29174 consolidated with A19502 (110th supplemental).** Read in full
(11 pp.; OCR good; caption and signature page checked on the image). [O][ocr][img]

- **Caption** (p. 1) [img]: "for authority to substitute motor coaches for its No. 1 East 14th
  Street and No. 2 San Pablo Avenue, No. 4 Shattuck Avenue and No. 5 Telegraph Avenue and No. 5
  Grove Street Transit Rail Lines, all in the County of Alameda".
- **The order's line names** (p. 9) [ocr]: "discontinue street railroad service on its No. 1, East
  14th Street Line, No. 2, San Pablo Line, No. 4, Shattuck Avenue Line, and No. 5,
  Telegraph Avenue-Grove Street Line".
  - **Correction to the plan:** in 1948 there was **no separate line 3**. The Grove St service was
    part of line 5, "Telegraph Avenue-Grove Street". CPTDB's "Line 3 … via Grove St (1904-1948)"
    [W] doesn't match the 1948 numbering. When the Grove St route became part of the No. 5 is
    unsourced here (see line 5 below).
- **Scale** (p. 3): "its four remaining local rail lines (Nos. 1, 2, 4 and 5)"; "The total length
  of the routes of the remaining rail lines, the Nos. 1, 2, 4 and 5 lines, is about 26 miles";
  local rail was "about 11 per cent" of the mileage, against "about 44 per cent" in 1938. [ocr]
- **Hearing:** "before Commissioner Rowell and Examiner Paul on April 30, 1948, and oral argument
  received on May 5, 1948" (p. 3). [ocr]
- **Recital of the other 1948 orders** (p. 2): "Subsequent to the issuance of said Decision No.
  41306 on March 9, 1948, the Commission granted two other applications … namely Decision No.
  41385, covering the No. 7 rail line in Berkeley, and Decision No. 41652 covering rail lines Nos.
  10, 11 and 12 in Oakland and Piedmont." [ocr]
- **Routes:** D42199 does **not** describe the rail routes. Its new bus streets ("only where
  needed", order (2), p. 10) hint at them [ocr]: "Along Grove Street between University Avenue and
  Adeline Street; … Along Adeline Street between Grove Street and Grove Street; … Along Grove
  Street between Adeline Street and San Pablo Avenue; … Along Telegraph Avenue between Durant
  Avenue and 22nd Street; … Along Shattuck Avenue between Durant Avenue and Telegraph Avenue;
  … Along 11th Street between Harrison and Fallon Street; … Along Fallon Street between 11th
  Street and 12th Street". These are bus grants, not rail descriptions; use them only as hints.
- **Dates** [img]: "The effective date of this order shall be 20 days from the date hereof";
  order (4): bus service "not later than 90 days after the effective date hereof"; order (1):
  rail may stop only "concurrently" with the buses; signed "this 4th day of November, 1948"
  (p. 11, handwritten).
  - **So the last day could fall between Nov 24, 1948 and Feb 22, 1949.** The plan's [W] Nov 28,
    1948 ("the last streetcar went out of operation") fits; see D42659 and the Tribune below.
- D42223 (Nov 16, 1948, A29174): "ORDER DENYING REHEARING", on the Bay Area Transportation
  League's petition against D42199 (p. 1) [ocr]. It didn't stay the order.

## 3. Lines 10, 11 and 12, 1948 (D41652)

**CPUC D41652, May 25, 1948, A29172.** Key System Transit Lines, "for authority to substitute
motor coaches for its Nos. 10 Piedmont Avenue, 11 Oakland Avenue, and 12 Grand Avenue - 16th
Street Rail Transit Lines" (caption, p. 1). Read in full (9 pp.; OCR good; footnotes and the
signature page checked on the images). [O][ocr][img]

- **Hearing:** "before Examiner Paul at Piedmont on March 31, 1948" (p. 2). [ocr]
- **The routes in service** (p. 1, footnote 1) [img]: "The street railroad lines proposed to be
  abandoned operate as follows:"
  - "The No. 10-Piedmont Avenue rail line is operated from Crocker Avenue in the vicinity of
    Hampton Road in the City of Piedmont along a private right of way, Highland Avenue, private
    right of way, Piedmont Avenue and Broadway to 9th Street in Oakland where a loop is made over
    9th Street, Franklin Street and 8th Street to Broadway."
  - "The No. 11-Oakland Avenue rail line is operated from 8th Street and Broadway in the City of
    Oakland along Broadway, 24th Street, Harrison Street, private right of way, Oakland Avenue,
    private right of way and Linda Avenue to Piedmont Avenue."
  - "The No. 12-Grand Avenue-16th Street rail line is operated from Oakland Avenue and Fairview
    Avenue in the City of Piedmont along Fairview Avenue, Grand Avenue, Webster Street, 14th
    Street, Market Street, 16th Street to the Southern Pacific Railroad Station near Wood Street
    in the City of Oakland."
- **The track given up** (p. 2, footnote 2) [img]: "The street railroad tracks, roadway and
  electrical overhead proposed to be abandoned are along the following streets:"
  - "From 15th Street and Broadway, via Broadway and Piedmont Avenue to 41st Street in the City of
    Oakland. By private right of way from Manor Drive to Highland Avenue, Highland Avenue and
    private right of way to Crocker Avenue in the City of Piedmont."
  - "From 24th Street and Broadway, 24th Street, Harrison Street, private right of way, Oakland
    Avenue, private right of way and Linda Avenue to Piedmont Avenue, in the Cities of Oakland
    and Piedmont."
  - "From MacArthur Boulevard and Grand Avenue, via Grand Avenue and Fairview Avenue to Oakland
    Avenue, in the City of Piedmont."
  - "From Southern Pacific Station in the vicinity of Wood Street, via 16th Street to Market
    Street, Market Street from 16th to 14th Streets, 14th Street from Market Street to Webster
    Street, Webster Street to Grand Avenue, in the City of Oakland."
  - Not in fn. 2, so kept for other lines: Broadway from 8th St to 15th St and the 9th–Franklin–8th
    loop; Broadway 15th–24th St (line 11 and others); **Piedmont Ave from 41st St to Manor Dr
    (shared with the Key System C line; compare key-system.md, the 1924 extension "over the
    present car tracks to Manor station")**; Grand Ave from Webster St to MacArthur Blvd.
- **Bus extension** (p. 2): "An extension of 6/10 of a mile beyond Crocker Avenue in Piedmont";
  "Moraga Road roughly parallels the No. 10 rail line at a distance of about 600 feet";
  bus "over 14th Street in Oakland between Market Street and Wood Street which is two blocks from
  the present route of operation of the No. 12-16th Street rail line". [ocr]
- **Dates** [img]/[ocr]: order (1)(b) rail may stop only "concurrently with the establishment of
  passenger stage service"; (4) buses "not later than 60 days after the effective date hereof";
  "This order shall be effective 20 days from the date hereof"; signed "this 25th day of May,
  1948" (p. 9, handwritten).
  - **So the last day could fall between June 14 and Aug 13, 1948.** The plan's [W] June 26/27,
    1948 fits the window.
- D42659 (Mar 29, 1949, A19502 110th suppl. & A29172 1st suppl.) footnote 1 confirms the six
  names: "No. 14 - East 18th Street, No. 15 - 14th Avenue-MacArthur Boulevard, No. 18 - Park
  Boulevard and Lakeshore Avenue, No. 10 - Piedmont Avenue, No. 11 - Oakland Avenue, No. 12 -
  Grand Avenue-16th Street" [img]; p. 2: "Complete motorization of all of applicant's East Bay
  local street rail lines has been accomplished. Abandonment of the last of the local street rail
  lines, Nos. 1, 2, 4 and 5 … was authorized by Decision No. 42199, dated November 4, 1948"; and
  the Oakland track-removal contract "on March 9, 1949" [img]. So rail ended **before Mar 29,
  1949**; no day given.

## 4. Line 6 (College Avenue) and the K (Alcatraz Avenue), 1946 (D39403)

**CRC D39403, Sept 10, 1946, A19502 (91st and 92nd supplementals).** Key System. Read in full
(7 pp.; OCR fair; the order (p. 5) and signature page (p. 7) checked on the images). This is the
"1946 College Ave decision" the plan couldn't find. [O][ocr][img]

- **What was asked** (p. 1): "to substitute motor coach operation for its existing street car
  service on its No. 6 College Avenue street car line and its 'K' Alcatraz street car line in the
  cities of Berkeley and Oakland" [ocr]. Hearing "in Berkeley on August 9, 1946" (p. 1) [ocr].
- **Line 6 route** (order I, p. 5) [img]: "Commencing with a loop around the block bounded by 9th,
  Franklin, 8th, and Broadway, Oakland, thence along Broadway to College Avenue, thence along
  College Avenue (passing through the boundary between Oakland and Berkeley) to Bancroft Way,
  thence along Bancroft Way to Fulton Street, thence along Fulton Street and Oxford Street to
  University Avenue, thence along University Avenue to Shattuck Avenue, thence along Shattuck
  Avenue to Bancroft Way, and thence along Bancroft Way to Fulton Street." (The same text is
  footnote 1, p. 1.)
- **K line route** (order I, p. 6) [ocr]: "Commencing at the intersection of Adeline Street and
  Alcatraz Avenue, thence along Alcatraz Avenue to College Avenue, thence along College Avenue to
  Bancroft Way, and thence along Bancroft Way to its intersection with Telegraph Avenue, all
  within the cities of Oakland and Berkeley." Its role (p. 2): it "serves as a connection with
  the 'F' Shattuck Avenue transbay train and its primary function is to handle transbay
  passengers originating at or destined to the College Avenue" [district]. [ocr]
- **Track to be removed** (p. 2): "all of the tracks on the College Avenue line between the
  intersection of Broadway and Piedmont Avenue in Oakland and the intersection of Bancroft Way and
  Telegraph Avenue in Berkeley, as well as the tracks on Alcatraz Avenue" [ocr]. So Broadway south
  of Piedmont Ave was shared (with line 10 and others).
- **Earlier track agreements** (p. 4): "Under Applications Nos. 26486 and 27097 the Commission
  approved agreements between the Key System and the cities of Berkeley and Oakland, which
  provided for the removal of the tracks involved herein and quit-claimed said tracks to the
  respective cities" [ocr].
- **Dates** [img]: abandonment "concurrently with the establishment of passenger stage service"
  (order I); "The authorization herein granted shall lapse and become void if not exercised within
  one (1) year from the date hereof"; "The effective date of this order shall be twenty (20) days
  from the date hereof"; signed "September 10, 1946" (handwritten; the file name agrees).
  - **So rail could end from Sept 30, 1946, and by Sept 10, 1947 at the latest.** The actual day
    is unsourced here. D40742 (Sept 23, 1947) speaks of "the No. 6 College Avenue Line" serving
    University Ave west to the SP station, which a streetcar never did on the D39403 route, so by
    then line 6 was a bus. [O][ocr]

## 5. Line 7 (Arlington Avenue–Euclid Avenue, Berkeley), 1948 (D41385, D37558)

**CPUC D41385, Mar 30, 1948, A29173.** Key System Transit Lines, "for authority to substitute
motor coach service for its No. 7 Arlington Avenue-Euclid Avenue transit rail line in the Counties
of Alameda and Contra Costa" (caption). Read in full (6 pp.; OCR fair; signature page on the
image). [O][ocr][img]

- **Ends** (pp. 1–2): "The rail line involved extends from Euclid Avenue and Regal Road in the City
  of Berkeley to a point on Arlington Avenue in the vicinity of Coventry Road in Contra Costa County
  about one block beyond the northerly boundary of the City of Berkeley." [ocr]
- **Hearing:** "before Examiner Paul at Berkeley on March 24, 1948" (p. 1). [ocr]
- Track: all of it to go "except those portions of said rails and overhead structures which are
  used for the operation of other rail lines"; portions "have been quitclaimed to the City of
  Berkeley", footnote: "Decision No. 37558 in Application No. 26486" (p. 2). [ocr]
- Track width (p. 3): the streets "in many cases, are narrow with a single track for operations in
  both directions". [ocr]
- **Dates** [img]: abandonment "concurrently with the establishment of passenger stage service"
  (order (1)); buses "not later than 30 days after the effective date hereof" (order (4)); "The
  effective date of this order shall be 10 days from the date hereof"; signed "this 30th day of
  March, 1948" (p. 6, handwritten).
  - **So rail could end between Apr 9 and May 9, 1948.** Day unsourced.
- D41385 doesn't give the streets between the ends. **D37558 does** (below).

**CRC D37558, Dec 19, 1944, A26486** (Key System, "to transfer certain operative properties to the
City of Berkeley"). 2 pp., read on the images. [O][img]

- p. 1: "The tracks referred to include those on the Arlington and Euclid Avenue lines and that
  portion of its line on College Avenue located within the City of Berkeley."
- p. 1: "Until that work is done Key System will retain full use of the tracks"; the agreement
  doesn't commit anyone "to the abandonment of rail service".
- **Order** (p. 2): quitclaim "the pavement located on the following streets:"
  - "a. College Avenue from the south city limits to the south line of Bancroft Way."
  - "b. Oxford Street from the north line of University Avenue to the end of the Euclid Avenue line

  - "c. Grove Street from the north line of University Avenue to the end of the Arlington Avenue
    line at the north city limits on Arlington Avenue."
  - "All of the above is shown in red on the map attached to the application as an exhibit" (the
    map isn't in the PDF).
- Signed "December 19, 1944" (handwritten), effective "the date hereof".

## 6. More on lines 14, 15, 18 and 6 in Oakland (D38438, 1945; D40055, 1947)

**CRC D38438, Nov 27, 1945, A27097** (Key System, "to transfer certain operative properties to the
City of Oakland"). 3 pp., order and signature read on the images. This is the decision D41306
cites. [O][img]

- p. 1 [ocr]: "The tracks referred to include those on 14th Avenue and MacArthur Boulevard;
  Broadway and College Avenue; East 21st Street; and Park Boulevard, as hereinafter more
  particularly described, all located within the City of Oakland"; Key System to pay "$244,000 in
  cash"; "Until that work is done Key System will retain full use of the tracks".
- **Order** (p. 2) [img]: quitclaim "the pavement located on the following streets:"
  - "A. East 18th Street from approximately Third Avenue to 14th Avenue, 14th Avenue from East 18th
    Street to MacArthur Boulevard, MacArthur Boulevard from 14th Avenue to 35th Avenue."
  - "B. Broadway from Piedmont Avenue to Clifton Street, College Avenue from Broadway to the
    northern city boundary of Oakland."
  - "C. East 21st Street from 14th Avenue to 25th Avenue, 25th Avenue from East 21st Street to
    East 24th Street, East 24th Street from 25th Avenue to 26th Avenue, 26th Avenue from East
    24th Street to East 27th Street, East 27th Street from 26th Avenue to Fruitvale Avenue."
  - "D. Park Boulevard from the vicinity of East 20th Street to Leimert Boulevard, Leimert
    Boulevard from Park Boulevard to the vicinity of Oakmore Road."
  - Maps: "shown in red attached to the application as Exhibit 'A'" (not in the PDF).
- Signed "this 27th day of November, 1945" (p. 3, handwritten).
- **What it adds:**
  - Line 18's Park Blvd track ran "from the vicinity of East 20th Street" to Leimert Blvd; with
    D41306 ("Park Boulevard and private right-of-way to East 18th Street") the private right-of-way
    ran from Park Blvd near E 20th St to E 18th St. Trace it.

  28, 1948" and falls inside D41306's window (Mar 19 – Apr 18).
- Same issue: hearings on lines 10–12 "were held March 31, and although no order has yet been
  issued the date of May 1st is expected for the end of car service on these and the No. 7 line".

### Lines 7, 10, 11 and 12: buses set for Sunday, June 20, 1948

- *The Western Railroader* 11(8), dated June 20, 1948 (`…_1948-06-20_11_8`) [P][ocr]: "KEY TO
  BUS-T FOUR LINES JUNE 20. The Public Utilities Commission having approved abandonment of the 10,
  11 and 12 car lines in Oakland provided rails and overhead remained intact for one year, the Key
  System set the date of June 20th for bus operation on the 7 (previously granted without
  restriction), 10, 11 and 12 lines. Application for busses on the 1, 2, 4 and 5 lines is still
  pending".
  - So the last cars probably ran **Saturday, June 19, 1948**. This is a **plan announced in
    advance**; no later issue (July–Sept 1948) confirms it. *The Western Railroader*, Aug 1948
    (`…_1948-08_11_10`): "The Euclid Avenue portion of Key System's former No. 7 street car line is
    being removed by a work train".
  - **Conflict:** the plan's [W] pointers give June 26 or 27 (or June 1) for lines 10–12. And for
    line 7, D41385 required buses by May 9, 1948 (30 days after the Apr 9 effective date), yet
    *The Western Railroader* puts line 7 into the June 20 change. Day for line 7: unresolved
    (between Apr 9 and June 20, 1948).

### Lines 1, 2, 4 and 5: buses from Sunday, Nov 28, 1948

- *The Western Railroader* 12(1), Nov 1948 (`…_1948-11_12_1`, p. 5), a reprinted newspaper
  clipping (paper not named) [P][ocr]: "The Public Utilities Commission's authorizing the Key System
  to substitute buses for streetcars on all remaining streetcar lines …"; "1—The East Fourteenth
  street …"; "No, 3 [sic]—San Pablo avenue line. No. 4—Shattuck avenue line. No. 5—Telegraph avenue
  - Grove street line. Key System officials said the bus service would be established Sunday,
  November 28." Headline: "Four Oakland Lines to Get Buses … Key Routes Affected: 1,3, 4 and 5".
  - The clipping's "No. 3—San Pablo" conflicts with D42199's "No. 2, San Pablo" (D42199 is the

  - Same issue: "November 21st the Bay Area Electric Railroad Association will operate its Key

- *The Western Railroader* 12(3), Jan 1949 [P][ocr]: "With the end of street car service in Oakland
  and Berkeley all cars were removed to the main Key System shops and stored at the Yerba Buena
  yards".
- So the **last local streetcars ran Saturday, Nov 27, 1948, with buses from Sunday, Nov 28**
  (announced; matches the plan's [W] "On November 28, 1948, the last streetcar went out of
  operation" and D42659's "Complete motorization … has been accomplished" by Mar 1949).

### Line 4 cut back to 8th St & Broadway (early 1947)

- *The Western Railroader* 10(3), Feb 1947 (`…_1947-02_10_3`) [P][ocr]: "KEY SYSTEM = The No 4,
  Shattuck Avenue, street car line was shortened with a new terminal at Eighth and Broadway. The

### Line 6 (College Ave): motorized by mid-1947

- *The Western Railroader* 10(9), Aug 1947 [P][ocr]: "The 700's were retired for a couple of weeks

### Richmond line: last cars Monday, Nov 6, 1933

  Ashby avenue, Berkeley, will be torn up"; "The first bus was scheduled to … reach Twenty-third
  street and Macdonald avenue … 6:02 this morning".

### Line 3 (Grove Street) merged into line 5, about the end of Feb 1946

- *The Western Railroader* 9(6), Apr 1946 (`…_1946-04_9_6`) [P][ocr]: "KEY SYSTEM PLANS TO MERGE
  BERKELEY LINES. The Key System has agreed to merge its Grove Street and Telegraph Avenue street

  Grove Streets, along Grove Street to 14th Street".
- After abandonment: *The Western Railroader*, Nov 1949 [P][ocr]: "In Berkely the Alcatraz and
  University Avenue tracks were recently removed and the current project is Grove Street"; in
  Oakland "the Webster Street trackage … being torn up recently".

### Oakland's line 7 (Foothill Blvd–East 12th St): buses set for July 31, 1938

- *The Western Railroader* 1(9), July 1938 (`…_1938-07_1_9`) [P][ocr]: "The East Bay Transit has
  announced that the number 7 Foothill Blvd.-East Twelfth Street rail line will be motorized

  line will run to the S. P. Sixteenth Street Station over the route formerly taken by the number
  7 line."
  - So the No. 7's name was "Foothill Blvd.-East Twelfth Street"; its west end ran to the SP 16th

  - From Aug 1938 line 14 was "West Twelfth Street and East Eighteenth Street". By 1947–48 it ended

- *The Western Railroader*, July 1945 [P][ocr], on the 1945 Oakland track agreements (D38438): they

  1935? route numbers)". The "#10" here is the later No. 15 (14th Ave–MacArthur Blvd); the editor
  flags the numbers as old ones. Don't rely on these numbers.

## 20. East Shore & Suburban: openings, 1904–1905, and the 1911–12 mergers

All from archive.org OCR text ([P][ocr]; *Oakland Tribune* = `oakland-tribune-YYYY-MM-DD`). Page
leaves aren't given: the `/stream/` text has no page breaks and the page index is blocked. Quotes
are from the Tribune's Richmond column unless noted.

### First line: July 28, 1904 (already in east-bay-electric.md; re-read)

- Tribune, July 29, 1904 ("NEW CAR COMPANY COMPLETES CONNECTION IN CONTRA COSTA TOWN"): "POINT
  RICHMOND, July 29.—The new line of the East Shore and Suburban Street Car Company, which forms
  the connecting link between the old and the new towns of Richmond, was opened yesterday"; Judge
  Wells: Richmond is "the possessor of the first electric street railway to be put in operation in
  Contra Costa county"; Rheem: "the officials of the company had already determined to extend the
  road to connect with the San Pablo avenue division of the Oakland Transit Company's system".
  - The route of this first line isn't described ("old and the new towns" = Point Richmond and the
    Macdonald Ave town). Tribune, Jan 7, 1905: "The trolley lines of the East Shore and Suburban
    road are being put in condition on Macdonald avenue."

### Richmond–Berkeley line on San Pablo Ave: built Dec 1904–Apr 1905; running by July 1905

- Tribune, July 4, 1904: the company "is preparing to extend its line from here to the Alameda
  county boundary and there connect with the line of the Oakland Transit Company"; routes surveyed
  "one via Stege and the other to follow the course of the San Pablo road".
- Tribune, Dec 3, 1904: "the commencement of the construction of the new road by the East Shore and
  Suburban Railroad Company, which will link Point Richmond to Oakland and Berkeley. Teams are
  delivering ties on Ohio street".
- Tribune, Jan 7, 1905: "Work on the new extension of the East Shore and Suburban Railroad to
  Berkeley is progressing rapidly and, weather permitting, the track should be completed in
  ninety days at the outside."
- Bay View Park real-estate ad (Stege, "1600 feet frontage on San Pablo Avenue"), dated issues:
  Mar 8, 1905 "THE EAST SHORE AND SUBURBAN ELECTRIC RAILWAY is now building its trolley line from
  Richmond to Berkeley"; Apr 8, 1905 "has now finished building its LINE and cars will soon be in
  operation from Richmond to Berkeley"; July 15, 1905 "has now finished building its [line] and
  cars are running directly past Bay View Park".
  - **So the Richmond–Berkeley (county line) service began between Apr 8 and July 15, 1905; first
    day unsourced.** (Ads are weak evidence; the wording changes at those dates.)
- Later references: Tribune, Nov 15, 1905: a new car "of a class similar to the one in use on the
  Southern Pacific-County line run"; Tribune, Aug 20, 1907: "taking the county line car on San
  Pablo avenue and transferring at the county line to the East Shore line".

### Stege branch: about July 1905

- Tribune, Apr 14, 1905: a contractor's camp "preparatory to building the East Shore and Suburban
  extension to Stege".
- Tribune, July 15, 1905: "The work of constructing the East Shore and Suburban branch line from San
  Pablo avenue to Stege is about completed, and it is expected the line will be in operation in a
  few days." Opening day unsourced; route unsourced.

### "San Pablo branch": opened Sunday, Sept 24, 1905

- Tribune, Wed Sept 27, 1905: "Five hundred passengers were carried over the San Pablo branch of the
  East Shore and Suburban Railway Sunday. This was the opening day of the line".
- Which line: probably the branch to the **town of San Pablo** (compare *Journal of Electricity,
  Power and Gas* 15(6), June 1905: "An electric railway franchise … to extend through Richmond to
  Stege and San Pablo, has been granted to the East Shore and Suburban Railway Company"), i.e. the
  later No. 29 to Church Lane (D24866). That identification is an inference.

### Other ES&S items

- Tribune, Apr 14, 1905: "the electric car line which will extend out Sixth street" (the later
  No. 28, "6th Street" line); Tribune, Feb 23 and Mar 2, 1906 ads: "the East Shore and Suburban's
  Ohio street car line". Openings unsourced.
- Tribune, June 28 and Nov 15, 1905: planned extensions to Point San Pablo/Point Molate "around the
  oil works"; not confirmed built.

### Mergers

- *Journal of Electricity, Power and Gas* 26(4), Jan 28, 1911: "The East Shore and Suburban Railway
  Company between Richmond in Contra Costa County and Oakland … was acquired last week by the
  United Properties Company, recently formed, with F. M. Smith as one of the leading factors."
- Tribune, Mar 21, 1912 ("Monster Combination of East Bay Traction Lines Is Completed"): "the
  biggest traction merger in the West, affecting 220 miles of electric roads about the bay, was
  completed with articles of incorporation filed here late yesterday in the name of the San
  Francisco-Oakland Terminal Railroads, including the Key Route, Oakland Traction Company, East
  Shore and Suburban Road and California Railway." So incorporation **Mar 20, 1912**.
- *Electric Railway Journal* 39(15), Apr 13, 1912, p. ~629 [ocr]: "On April 1, 1912, an announcement
  … regarding the personnel of the Oakland Traction Company, San Francisco, Oakland & San José
  Consolidated Railway, East Shore & Suburban Railway and California Railroad, which have been
  consolidated as the San Francisco-Oakland Terminal Railways"; "Traction division—formerly Oakland
  Traction Company, East Shore & Suburban Railway and California Railway".
  - **Name conflict:** "Terminal Railroads" (Tribune) vs "Terminal Railways" (ERJ). The ERJ form is
    the usual one.

## 21. Line 6 once included the Grove St–Arlington branch (D36144, 1943)

**CRC D36144, Feb 2, 1943, A19502 (59th supplemental)** (No. 67 Spruce St bus). 5 pp.; p. 3 read
on the image. [O][img]

- p. 3: "The proposed modification of route from Grove Street to Shattuck Avenue between Rose Street
  and University Avenue will eliminate duplicate service by the No. 67 Spruce Street motor coach
  line and the No. 6 street railway line, and supply additional local service over a less
  congested route on Shattuck Avenue which is now served only by the 'F' Berkeley Shattuck Avenue
  transbay rail line."
- So in **Feb 1943 the No. 6 ran on Grove St between University Ave and Rose St**, i.e. the College
  Ave line continued through downtown Berkeley onto the Grove St–Arlington branch (CPTDB's
  "Downtown Oakland - Berkeley Arlington Ave via College Ave" [W] agrees).
- By 1946 the Arlington and Euclid ends were separate: *The Western Railroader*, Apr 1946 [P][ocr]:
  "Trolley coaches for Arlington, Euclid and College Avenue lines are beginning to arrive"; D39403
  (Sept 1946) describes line 6 without Grove St; D41385 (1948) calls the remainder "No. 7
  Arlington Avenue-Euclid Avenue". When the split happened is unsourced.
- p. 1 [img]: the No. 67 bus then ran "to The Alameda, to Grove Street, to University Avenue, to
  Oxford Street, to Rose Street" (bus route, for context).
