# Research notes: the Bay Bridge railway, 1939–1958

Gathered Oct 14, 2026 for the Phase 2a pilot: the trail behind `data/past/bay-bridge/`. Every fact in
the data cites its source in `data/sources.json`.

## Ownership, operation, dates

- **Built** by the State of California, Department of Public Works, Bay Bridge Division (C. H.
  Purcell, chief engineer). California Highways and Public Works (CHPW), Jan 1939, p. 16: "The Bridge
  Railway was constructed by the State Department of Public Works under the direction of Chief
  Engineer C. H. Purcell."
- **Financed** by the California Toll Bridge Authority (CTBA) with Reconstruction Finance Corporation
  bonds, repaid by tolls of 2½ cents a passenger (Railway Signaling, Mar 1939, p. 154). HAER CA-32
  p. 212: the state "paid for all improvements to the railroad approaches and in construction of the
  San Francisco loop and Transbay Terminal."
- **Owned** by the state; the CTBA took possession on Jan 14, 1939 and turned it over to "Interurban
  Electric, Key System, and Sacramento-Northern" (CHPW, Feb 1939, pp. 6–8). The railways ran it
  through a board of governors and a superintendent. CPUC D54669 (Mar 12, 1957) p. 4 calls it "the
  state-owned Bridge Railway, including the San Francisco Terminal and its rail approaches."
- **Agreements**: with the Key System and Southern Pacific Mar 6, 1936; with the Sacramento Northern,
  Sept 1938 (HAER CA-32 pp. 211–212; CRC D29614).
- **Milestones** (CHPW Feb 1939 p. 28; Dec 1937 p. 23): first tie Nov 29, 1937 at span E-22; first
  test train (Key) Sept 23, 1938; first trains into the San Francisco terminal Dec 14 (Key), Dec 17
  (IER), Dec 20, 1938 (SN test); third rail energized Jan 5, 1939. HAER puts the first tie at the
  Oakland approach; the contemporary CHPW account is preferred.
- **Dedicated** Jan 14, 1939; **first regular service Jan 15, 1939** (Railway Signaling p. 154: "Train
  service over the bridge was placed in operation on Jan. 15"; HAER CA-32 p. 212).
- **IER's end**: its Alameda lines replaced from Jan 18, 1941 (CRC D33891); its last transbay lines,
  Shattuck Avenue and Ninth Street, replaced by Key System service "established on July 26, 1941"
  (CRC D34460 p. 1). HAER CA-164's "July 4, 1940" is contradicted by the CRC record.
- **Sacramento Northern's end**: "early 1941" (HAER CA-32 p. 212); the day is unsourced.
- **Key System's end**: CPUC D54669 (Mar 12, 1957) authorized it "to discontinue transbay rail service
  and to substitute motor coach service"; "The last trains crossed the Bay Bridge in 1958" (HAER CA-32
  p. 215). April 20, 1958 is given only by secondary sources (SF Chronicle, 2014).
- **Removal**: buses used the loop and terminal "by July 1959"; the train lanes were paved in
  1959–61; the reconstructed bridge opened to one-way traffic on each deck Oct 12, 1963 (HAER CA-32
  pp. 30, 232–234).

## On the bridge

- Two tracks on the lower deck (Railway Signaling p. 148), on its **south half** (HAER CA-32 p. 231;
  in the Yerba Buena Island tunnel, "The railroad lanes on the south side of the tunnel," p. 233).
- Interlocking limits: from "the west end of the main bridge structure on Rincon hill to Yerba Buena
  island … 11,400 ft., and between the island anchorage and the Oakland interlocking limits at the
  east end of the main structure, 12,400 ft." (p. 148); 4.5 miles in all.

## San Francisco end

- HAER CA-32 pp. 14–15: "The trains came off of the lower deck between First and Second Streets and
  looped through the Transbay Transit Terminal at Mission Street, located between Fremont and First
  Street, and then back onto the bridge."
- Railway Signaling p. 150: "The bridge ends on Rincon hill near Bryant street … From the end of the
  bridge, the two tracks curve to the north and, together with a third track, extend two blocks to
  Folsom street, at which point the tracks form a large loop with a six-track, double-end station on
  the north side of the loop. This station faces Mission street … All trains normally run
  counter-clockwise in this loop, thus arriving from the east end and departing from the west end."
- The loop: "an elevated concrete and steel structure that looped some two-thirds of a mile …
  3,439 foot elevated structure" (HAER CA-32 p. 31). Six station tracks, 700 ft long; IER on the three
  north tracks, Key and SN on the other three (Railway Signaling p. 150).
- The USGS 1950 San Francisco North sheet draws the loop ("KEY SYSTEM") and the "Key System Terminal".

## East Bay end

- The bridge "touched down on what had been a train yard island, or 'mole'" (HAER CA-32 p. 29). The
  rail left the bridge on its south side (HAER CA-32 sheet 2).
- Railway Signaling p. 150: the old Key System layout was "entirely rebuilt … to provide connections
  from the bridge tracks to inbound and outbound tracks of both roads, without crossings at grade, but
  also to provide a yard for each road … constructed on the mole formerly used by the Key System …
  a track layout about two miles long."
- HAER CA-164 pp. 2, 5–6: the yards and shops were "adjacent to the East Bay approach to the Bridge,
  on the south side of the Key System Mole." "The Interurban Electric Railway trains used a
  double-tracked lead from the Bridge Yards to a Y-shaped viaduct near the foot of 26th Street … the
  point of divergence was named 26th Street Junction." Seventh Street and Alameda trains joined the
  old Berkeley main line at 22nd Street; Berkeley trains joined it at 32nd Street.
- On the USGS 1949 Oakland West sheet, "KEY SYSTEM" labels the track just south of the bridge
  approach, and "TRANSIT LINES" the multi-track line east of the toll plaza.
