# Research: the East Bay and North Bay electric railways (Phase 2b.D)

Gathered on Oct 14, 2026, for the past era. Working files (decision PDFs and their text, journal
issues) are outside the repo; the quotes below carry their sources.

**How it was searched.** archive.org full-text search (the Oakland Tribune is indexed from about
1890), CPUC legacy decision PDFs and journal PDFs read as text. "Leaf" is archive.org's 0-based scan
leaf. Newspaper quotes were cut at about 125 characters by the fetch tool, and "FTS snippet" means
the quote is only the search highlight: **read every newspaper quote on the page image before it
goes on the map.** CDNC, chroniclingamerica.loc.gov and HathiTrust were blocked.

**Tags:** [secondary] or [pointer] marks Wikipedia, railfan books and magazines.

## Summary

Three kinds of source carry most of the new findings:

1. **SP and IER employee timetables** (California State Railroad Museum scans on archive.org,
   `cscrm_2021_02_*`): station lists, junctions, towers and line names. The IER's Time Table No. 1,
   "To Take Effect Sunday, January 15, 1939, at 4:01 A. M.", is the first primary evidence of when
   the IER began (the pilot's conflict 7).
2. **The Oakland Tribune on archive.org:**
   - the Seventh Street line reached Dutton Avenue on Oct 1, 1913;
   - Melrose–Havenscourt trains were "now running" by Jan 11–12, 1913;
   - Ninth Street loop service was announced for Monday, Feb 19, 1912 (a third date, conflicting);
   - the East Shore & Suburban opened July 28, 1904.
3. **Routes as streets, from CRC/CPUC decisions:** Richmond line (D26438, 1933), Richmond lines 28
   and 29 (D24866, 1932), Key lines 10, 11 and 12 (D41652, 1948) and the ends of 14, 15 and 18
   (D41306, 1948), the P&SR's Fourth Street line in Santa Rosa (D24310, 1931), and the SN and NWP
   passenger abandonments (D33009, D34232, D34457).

## 1. The pilot's open items

### Key System E (Claremont line)

- **Opened in sections** (Demoro, FTS snippet [secondary]).
- **The 55th Street branch, ferry to Telegraph Ave:**
  - Tribune, June 17, 1905, leaf 15: "service just about to be started on the Fifty-fifth street
    branch of the Key route, which, for the present, will run from the ferry to Telegraph avenue, on
    Fifty-fifth street".
  - Tribune, Nov 10, 1905, leaf 3: "the Fifty-fifth street line, which has already been built as far
    as Telegraph avenue, will be extended to Claremont avenue and out Claremont to College avenue."
  - Running by Nov 1, 1907 (Tribune, leaf 13). **The first day is unsourced** (between June 1905
    and Nov 1907).
- **The extension to Claremont:** Tribune, Oct 25, 1912, leaf 9, a retrospective: "In 1908 the
  Claremont line was opened". Only the year is sourced.
- **Closed:** 1958; CPUC D50620 and D50731 put night and Sunday service on buses from Nov 7, 1954.
- **Route:**
  - The 1911 Polk-Husted directory puts 53rd St "2 s of Key Route Ry Claremont line", so the line ran
    on 55th St.
  - D50731, p. 1: "between the grounds of the Hotel Claremont at Domingo Avenue in the City of
    Berkeley and the said bridge terminal a distance of 11.14 miles".
  - The 1954 bus, "closely paralleling the rail routes": "55th Street from Market Street to Vincente
    Street. … Vincente Street from 55th Street to 56th Street. … 56th Street from Vincente Street to
    Claremont Avenue. … Claremont Avenue from 56th Street to Russell Street."
- **Placeable:** 55th St from about Market St to Telegraph Ave, and Claremont Ave from 56th St to
  Russell St. **Must be traced:** the hotel-grounds tail, and everything west of Market St (the E had
  its own track there; "Until 1933, the Northbrae line branched off from the Claremont line",
  Demoro), which also explains the pilot's gap from 54th St and Adeline St to Sacramento St.

### Key System G (Westbrae shuttle)

- Opened Nov 15, 1911; closed July 26, 1941 (both already in the pilot).
- CRC D34389 (July 3, 1941), order VIII(b): "Westbrae Shuttle Rail Line 'G' operating between a
  point of connection with the Sacramento Street Rail Line 'H' in the vicinity of University Avenue
  in Berkeley, and the end of the line located in the vicinity of Santa Fe Avenue."
- **Junction conflict:** the 1911 Tribune puts it "at Virginia"; D34389 near University Ave.
- The right-of-way is private: trace it (USGS 1915 or a Sanborn map).
- D34389, p. 6, on the H: "proceeds principally over private right of way through Berkeley, along
  Sacramento Street and Monterey Avenue to a terminus at Colusa Street".

### SP Eighteenth Street line (Oakland)

- **Opened March 1912** (Tribune, Mar 28 and 29, 1912: "A 5c fare from Fourteenth and Franklin
  streets to Sixteenth Street Depot is about to be inaugurated on the new Eighteenth-street Southern
  Pacific Loop"). The first day is unconfirmed. Closed Mar 26, 1933 (CRC D27800, in the pilot).
- **Route:** CRC D17743 names 18th St, 20th St and Franklin St, westbound on 21st St and "Jones
  Street"; from 1926 on Webster St. No Jones St exists in Oakland today; the link from 18th St to
  20th/21st Sts must be traced.

### SP "Alameda via Fruitvale" (horseshoe) line

- SP employee timetable No. 12 (Aug 1, 1937), pp. 13–18: stations Melrose, Fruitvale, 23rd Ave,
  East Oakland; "Drawbridge Interlocked: Fruitvale Ave. between Fruitvale and Lincoln Junction";
  "Lincoln Ave. Line: Both tracks Fruitvale Bridge to Mastick Junction, to Tynan to Alameda Pier";
  "Blanding Tower—Alameda. South Fruitvale Bridge and Blanding Avenue."
- **Closed:** gone by Jan 15, 1939 (absent from IER Time Table No. 1). **Opened:** unsourced.
- Trace from the USGS 1949 sheets.

### SP Webster Street line

- Opened June 29, 1911; closed Dec 27, 1923 (both in the pilot).
- SP employee timetable No. 2 (Nov 1, 1914), pp. 29–34: "OAKLAND (14th and Franklin)—Via Webster
  Street"; "operate single track on Webster Street between Oakland (14th and Franklin) and First
  Street"; towers at First and Webster and Third and Webster; the Harrison Street drawbridge; Webster
  Junction tower in Alameda.
- Webster St from 14th St to 1st St is placeable; the drawbridge and Webster Junction must be traced.

### SP Seventh Street line, Melrose to 106th Ave and Dutton Ave

- **Streets:** CRC D17743 names Bond St, Beck St, Blanche St and Bancroft Ave; Beck and Blanche Sts
  don't exist today. Trace from the 1948/49 USGS Oakland East and San Leandro sheets.
- **Stations** (timetable No. 12, 1937): Melrose, Seminary Ave, Havenscourt, 82nd Ave, "103rd Ave.
  (Jct. Switch)", Dutton Ave.
- **Dates:**
  - Melrose–Havenscourt: running by **Jan 11–12, 1913** (Tribune ads: "trains now running"; "The
    Southern Pacific Melrose train now runs right into Havenscourt"). A celebration "to mark the
    completion of the Southern Pacific electric service" was planned in Dec 1912 (Tribune, Dec 13
    and 16, 1912).
  - Havenscourt–Dutton Ave: **Oct 1, 1913**, first train (Tribune, Sept 30, 1913, leaf 8: "First
    Train of Southern Pacific Will Go Over Route Tomorrow", "timed to arrive at Dutton avenue about 6
    o'clock in the morning"; Oct 1, 1913: owners "this morning piled rocks and ties on the track").

### SP Ninth Street line, opening

- Tribune, Feb 12, 1912, leaf 9: "Berkeley Electric Trains to Begin Running on Ninth Street Week From
  Today"; "Beginning next Monday trains are to make the circuit of the loop". That makes **Monday,
  Feb 19, 1912** the announced start, against the SF Call (Feb 3) and Wikipedia (Feb 7). Unconfirmed.

### When the IER began

- `cscrm_2021_02_001411`, "Interurban Electric Railway 1/15/1939 Number 1", leaf 0: "To Take Effect
  Sunday, January 15, 1939, at 4:01 A. M." (OCR reads "1989").

## 2. Sacramento Northern

Succession: the Oakland & Antioch built Bay Point–Oakland; the Oakland, Antioch & Eastern built Bay
Point–Sacramento; then the San Francisco–Sacramento RR and the Sacramento Northern.

### Route out of Oakland

- Journal of Electricity, Power and Gas 28(10), Mar 9, 1912, p. 217: "From the Key Route junction at
  Fortieth street and Shafter avenue, the Oakland and Antioch runs for about 1¾ miles along Shafter
  avenue".
- Electric Railway Journal 41(16), Apr 19, 1913, p. 726: "From the main Oakland depot at Fortieth
  Street and Shafter Avenue … extends up Shafter Avenue and crosses the north arm of Lake Temescal.
  Thence it winds up Shepherd Canyon on a 3 per cent grade to the Redwood Peak Tunnel, which pierces
  the Coast Range for a distance of 3458 ft." Trains used "the Key Route tracks upon the 2-mile Key
  Route pier".
- Placeable: Shafter Ave from 40th St to near College Ave (about 1.4 miles today, against 1912's
  "1¾"). Beyond: trace from the USGS sheets.

### Openings

| Stretch | Date | Source |

| Bay Point–Concord | first train Feb 1911 (probably Feb 13) | Tribune, Feb 14, 1911, leaf 4 |
| Bay Point–Walnut Creek | about Sept 1911 | JOEPG, Mar 9, 1912, p. 217 ("put in commercial operation about six months ago"); conflict: May 4, 1911 (NRHS bulletin) [secondary] |

### Endings

- **Bay Bridge service** (from Jan 15, 1939): CRC D34232 (May 27, 1941) authorized ending
  "passenger train service between the City and County of San Francisco and Pittsburg", "concurrently
  with and not before" augmented Greyhound schedules. Last day June 30, 1941, from a 1945
  reminiscence [secondary]; no primary source.
- **North of West Pittsburg:** CRC D33009 (Apr 16, 1940) authorized discontinuance "on July 30, 1940,
  unless sooner expressly permitted" (main line to Chico, Woodland and Colusa branches); D33416 (Aug
  20, 1940) followed. The actual last day is unsourced.
- **Lafayette to 40th & Shafter** (freight only from 1941): *Western Railroader*, Aug 1956: ICC
  approval to abandon "between Lafayette and 40th & Shafter including the trackage in Shafter
  Avenue"; *Orinda Sun*, Mar 1, 1957: "expected to abandon the line from Lafayette to Oakland in
  March." Closed 1957, expected in March.
- **West Pittsburg–Montezuma and the ferry:** CPUC D56165 (Jan 28, 1958) recites ICC authority of
  Sept 27, 1955 (Finance Docket 18617).

## 3. Richmond, Oakland and Alameda

### East Shore & Suburban (Richmond)

- **Opened July 28, 1904:** Tribune, July 29, 1904, leaf 13: "The new line of the East Shore and
  Suburban Street Car Company, which forms the connecting link between the old and the new towns of
  Richmond, was opened yesterday".
- Merged into the Oakland Traction in 1912 (ERJ, Apr 13, 1912).
- **Richmond line**, CRC D26438 (Oct 16, 1933): "From a point fifty (50) feet south of the
  intersection of Ashby and San Pablo Avenues in the City of Berkeley, northerly along San Pablo
  Avenue to Potrero Avenue, westerly along Potrero Avenue to Pullman Avenue, northerly along Pullman
  Avenue to Twenty-third Street, northerly along Twenty-third Street to Macdonald Avenue, westerly
  along Macdonald Avenue to Garrard Boulevard, westerly along Garrard Boulevard to Standard Avenue,
  northerly along Standard Avenue to Scofield Avenue, westerly along Scofield Avenue to Western Drive,
  northerly along Western Drive and private right of way to a point near the Richmond Terminal of The
  Richmond and San Rafael Ferry". Buses from Nov 7, 1933 (*Richmond Record-Herald*, Nov 3, 1933), so
  the last cars ran Nov 6.
- **Line 28** and **line 29**, CRC D24866 (June 13, 1932): routes on 14th St, Ohio Ave, 6th St,
  Barrett Ave and 8th St (28), and Macdonald Ave, 23rd St and Market St to Church Lane (29). Last
  days unsourced (Wikipedia: Aug 1, 1932).

### Oakland Traction and its precursors (anchors only)

- Cable lines: Oakland Cable Railway on San Pablo Ave (1886–1899, from Seventh and Broadway)
  [secondary, Hilton p. 355]; Consolidated Piedmont Cable Co. (Tribune, Aug 8, 1890; Hilton p. 357).
- 1948 abandonments: D41652 (May 25, 1948) gives lines 10, 11 and 12 street by street; D41306 (Mar
  9, 1948) gives lines 14, 15 and 18; D42199 (Nov 4, 1948) the last four lines (1, 2, 4 and 5). The
  last streetcar day is unsourced.
- **Proposed phasing:** first the 1930s–1948 network from the abandonment decisions (fetch D41385,
  D26059, D28615 and the 1946 College Ave decision), then earlier lines from franchise ordinances,
  directories and timetables, then the cable lines from Hilton.

### Alameda Belt Line and Oakland Terminal Railway

- Belt Line: bought from the city of Alameda for $30,000 and extended "from a connection with the
  existing track of the city's railroad in Clement avenue near Minturn street" (CRC Decisions vol. 26,
  1925, pp. 808–809); owned by the Western Pacific and Santa Fe; closed Nov 10, 1998 [secondary].
- Oakland Terminal: *Western Railroader*, Aug 1956: "The new 2-mile line will run from the Key System
  tracks at 12th and Poplar over Union Street to the Western Pacific tracks at Third and Union."

## 4. North Bay

Drawn so far: the NWP's electric lines in Marin (`data/past/nwp-marin/`), from the Street Railway
Journal and D34457 above, traced from the 1941 USGS Tamalpais sheet.

- **NWP electric lines in Marin** (North Shore Railroad until 1907), third rail: *Street Railway
  Journal* 23(1), Jan 2, 1904, pp. 4–7: Mill Valley branch "placed in operation on Aug. 21" (1903),
  main line to San Rafael "opened on Oct. 17" (1903); Sausalito–San Rafael 11.95 miles. **Closed:**
  CRC D34457 (July 29, 1941): "The pooling arrangement was canceled March 1, 1941 at which time
  applicant also discontinued all service on its interurban electric line between Sausalito and San
  Rafael and on its ferry route"; last trains Feb 28, 1941.
- **Petaluma & Santa Rosa:** service "established" by Dec 10, 1904 (Electrical Review); further dates
  Wikipedia only. CRC D24818 (May 31, 1932) authorized ending passenger service (last day unsourced).
  CRC D24310 (Dec 14, 1931): abandonment of "its line of railroad on Fourth Street, from east of the
  west line of Wilson Street to its terminus at Fourth and McDonald Streets" in Santa Rosa.
- Santa Rosa and Petaluma horsecars, San Rafael: nothing primary found.

## Conflicts

1. Ninth Street line opening: Feb 3, 1912 (SF Call), Feb 7 (Wikipedia) or Feb 19 (Tribune).
2. Westbrae junction: at Virginia St (1911) or near University Ave (1941).
3. Havenscourt–Dutton Ave: Oct 1, 1913 (Tribune), not July 1912 (Wikipedia).
4. Havenscourt opening: Dec 13, 1912 (Wikipedia) against "now running" on Jan 11, 1913 (Tribune).
5. Bay Point–Walnut Creek: about Sept 1911 (JOEPG) or May 4, 1911 (NRHS bulletin).
6. The OA&E summit tunnel: 3,200 ft, 3,458 ft or "two-thirds of a mile"; "Redwood Peak" or
   "Shepard Pass".
7. OA&E Oakland–Bay Point: formal opening planned for Apr 5; regular service from Apr 7, 1913.
8. Bay Point–Sacramento: regular service planned for Aug 15; schedule from Sept 3, 1913.
9. SN north of West Pittsburg: D33009's July 30, 1940, then D33416; the actual day is unknown.
10. Shafter Ave: 1¾ miles (1912) against about 1.4 miles today.
11. Key line 15: abandoned 1946 (Wikipedia) against D41306 (1948).
