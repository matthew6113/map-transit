# Research notes: the Key System transbay trains, 1903–1958

Gathered Oct 14, 2026 for the Phase 2a pilot: the trail behind `data/past/key-system/`. Every fact in
the data cites its source in `data/sources.json`, with pages. "Q:" marks a verbatim quote (OCR, with
obvious OCR errors fixed). Oakland Tribune pages are archive.org scan leaves (from 0); the printed
page is unverified. "Demoro snippet" is a line from archive.org's full-text search of the book
(borrow-only); no page was visible.

## Ferry trunk (1903–1939)

- Pier from the Oakland shore to the ferry depot. Street Railway Journal (SRJ), Feb 13, 1904, p. 247:
  "A pier, over 3 miles in length, has been constructed from the Oakland shore"; p. 249: "the pier has
  a length of 16,240 ft." (elsewhere "the total length to the end of the wharf is 16,400 ft.").
- Subway under the SP main line at the land end. SRJ Feb 13, 1904, p. 251: "In order to avoid a grade
  crossing with the Southern Pacific Company's main tracks at the land end of the pier"; "The total
  length of the subway is 1070 ft".
- Yerba Buena Avenue. SRJ p. 251: "For 2980 ft. east of the subway the double tracks are carried on
  Yerba Buena Avenue to the station at the crossing of San Pablo Avenue."
- Branches (SRJ p. 251): "the Berkeley line branches off and runs nearly north"; "The Piedmont branch
  is being constructed east from San Pablo Avenue on Fortieth Avenue" (the Tribune says Fortieth
  Street); "the Oakland branch will run south on San Pablo Avenue to Fourteenth Street and Broadway"
  (a plan, built differently).
- Later: Yerba Buena Avenue runs "from Adeline Street to 40th Street" (CPUC D50731, Nov 3, 1954, p. 2).
- First regular service: SRJ Feb 20, 1904, p. 283: "The first regular train service over the Key Route
  was inaugurated on Oct. 26" (1903, implied).
- Power house on Yerba Buena Avenue (Oakland Tribune, Oct 26, 1904; SRJ 1906 photo caption "YERBA
  BUENA POWER STATION OF THE KEY ROUTE, OAKLAND"). The Emeryville shops: unsourced.

## Piedmont line (C)

- East on 40th Street from the trunk at San Pablo Avenue (Tribune, Feb 4, 1904: "complete the Piedmont
  branch along Fortieth street"); terminal 1904–1924 "on Piedmont avenue, between Fortieth and
  Forty-first streets" (Tribune, May 28, 1904).
- Opened June 1, 1904: Tribune May 28, 1904 (Saturday): "Wednesday next at 10 a.m. the Key Route will
  inaugurate its ferry system in Piedmont"; Tribune June 1, 1904: "this morning. The event was the
  reception of the Key route system".
- Extension to Manor station, Nov 3, 1924 (Tribune, Nov 3, 1924, leaf 16: "first opportunity today to
  use the new extension … over the present car tracks to Manor station"); Wikipedia gives Nov 21, 1924
  (Demoro p. 58). Streets of the extension relayed via Wikipedia only.
- 1954 terminus: "Oakland Avenue near Fairview Avenue in the City of Piedmont" (CPUC D50731, p. 1).

## 22nd Street line (B)

- Trunk to Poplar Street, south on Poplar, east on 22nd Street (today's West Grand Avenue) to
  Broadway (CPUC D49307, Nov 10, 1953, p. 2: "from Yerba Buena Avenue along Poplar Street and West
  Grand Avenue"; Tribune June 12, 1911: "Poplar Junction").
- Opened May 16, 1906 (SRJ June 2, 1906, p. 848: "The Twenty-Second Street or Oakland branch … was opened
  for regular traffic on May 16th"; "The terminus of the new line is at Broadway and Twenty-Second
  Street").
- East of Broadway (1919–20), Lakeshore and Trestle Glen: relayed via Wikipedia (Demoro pp. 52–54).
  1950s termini: Wesley Station (Trestle Glen Road and Lakeshore Avenue) and Underhills Station
  (D49307, p. 2; D54242, Dec 11, 1956, p. 2).

## Claremont line (E)

- 55th Street from about Linden Street to Telegraph Avenue, then Claremont Avenue (Demoro snippets
  only). Terminus "between the grounds of the Hotel Claremont at Domingo Avenue in the City of Berkeley
  and the said bridge terminal a distance of 11.14 miles" (D50731, p. 1). Dates relayed via Wikipedia
  only (Demoro pp. 33, 138).

## Berkeley line (F)

- 1903: from San Pablo Avenue "nearly north"; "crosses Temescal Creek on Linden Street, on the short
  trestle" (SRJ Feb 13, 1904, p. 252); "From Lorin station to Berkeley the Key Route parallels the
  Berkeley and Berryman local tracks of the Southern Pacific" (p. 251); terminus "at Center Street and
  Shattuck Avenue" (p. 251). Opened with the first service, Oct 26, 1903.
- By 1933 the terminus was University Avenue (CRC D25740, Mar 16, 1933, p. 8). D25740 (order I-A,
  p. 10) let it discontinue the line "along Adeline Street and Shattuck Avenue between the
  intersection of Adeline Street and Alcatraz Avenue and the end of the line at University Avenue".
  The day it was cut back is relayed via Wikipedia only ("after March 31, 1933").
- July 26, 1941: back to University Avenue (D34460, p. 1; route in D34389 order III, p. 13: "from the
  intersection of Adeline Street and Alcatraz Avenue, along Adeline Street to Shattuck Avenue, Shattuck
  Avenue to Shattuck Avenue (East), and along Shattuck Avenue (East) to a terminus at University
  Avenue").
- August 6, 1941: to Northbrae Station, Sutter and Hopkins, over the former IER tracks (D34460 order I,
  p. 12; D35900, Oct 27, 1942, pp. 2–3: "This service was initiated August 6, 1941").
- Through the Northbrae Tunnel to The Alameda: denied (D34999, Feb 3, 1942), then ordered "within
  thirty (30) days" (D35900, p. 7), "a distance of some 1500 feet, including the Northbrae Tunnel"
  (p. 2). The opening, Dec 6, 1942, is relayed via Wikipedia only.

## Sacramento Street line (H) and Westbrae (G)

- Tribune Nov 13, 1911, leaf 12: "The new Berkeley main line of the Key Route extends from Fifty-fourth
  and Adeline streets, Oakland, along Sacramento street to Hopkins street in Berkeley. Along Hopkins
  street an extension runs through a private right of way to Northbrae"; "From Sacramento street, at
  Virginia, another Key Route line branches off to the fast growing town of Albany, through a private
  right of way".
- Opened Nov 15, 1911 (Tribune Nov 15, 1911, leaf 12: "Operation of the Sacramento street, Northbrae
  and Albany lines of the Key Route began this morning").
- 1933: the Hopkins Street end (to El Dorado Avenue) given up, and the H line moved onto SP's tracks
  on Monterey Avenue to Colusa Avenue (D25740 orders I-B and II, p. 10). Implementation date unsourced.
- Both converted to buses July 26, 1941 (D34460, p. 10; D34389 order VIII).

## 12th Street line (A)

- Through service established June 11, 1911, "the Poplar street route into Twelfth street and
  terminating for the present at Broadway" (Tribune June 12, 1911).
- Before Mar 22, 1941 the terminus was 1st Avenue and East 14th Street; extended that day along East
  14th Street to 105th Avenue (D33891 order V; D34060, pp. 3–4); then in April 1941 onto the former IER
  Seventh Street line tracks to Havenscourt (D34060 order III).
- June 7, 1950: authorized to drop service east of 12th and Oak Streets (D44289, pp. 9–10); the
  changeover day (Oct 29, 1950) is relayed via Wikipedia only.

## Bay Bridge service and the end

- Bridge rail service Jan 15, 1939 (see bay-bridge-railway.md).
- D54669 (Mar 12, 1957) authorized the end of transbay rail service on lines A, B, C, E and F; later
  orders held it up (D56016, Dec 30, 1957); the lifting order wasn't found. Last trains Apr 19/20,
  1958, relayed via Wikipedia only.

## Conflicts

1. Piedmont branch street: SRJ "Fortieth Avenue", Tribune "Fortieth street". Use 40th Street.
2. Northbrae: Northbrae Station opened Aug 6, 1941; the tunnel to The Alameda was ordered Oct 27, 1942
   (opening relayed as Dec 6, 1942). Wikipedia's "1941" for the tunnel is wrong.
3. Piedmont extension: Nov 3, 1924 (Tribune) vs Nov 21, 1924 (Demoro, relayed).
4. Westbrae junction: "at Virginia" (Tribune, 1911) vs University Avenue (Demoro; D34389, 1941).
   Opening Nov 15, 1911 (Tribune) vs "December" (Demoro snippet).
5. Lakeshore and Underhills dates (1919, Nov 8, 1920) are relayed; 1920 Tribune ads suggest trains
   ran earlier.
6. Shared Shattuck track: Wikipedia says from 1930; D25740 (1933) describes separate parallel tracks.
7. Pier length: 16,240 ft vs 16,400 ft to the end of the wharf (SRJ, same article).
8. Last rail day: Apr 19 or 20, 1958 (both relayed).
