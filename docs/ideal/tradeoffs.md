# The ideal network: proposals and their tradeoffs

For Matthew, Oct 16, 2026 (Phase 4). The ideal network is yours to design (CLAUDE.md): this page
lays out the serious proposals it could draw on, grouped by the choices they pose, with what their
own documents say each one costs, carries and gives up. It recommends nothing. Pick, change or add
lines, then draw them with the editor (below).

**Where the facts come from.** The proposals' own documents, as web search showed them on their
publishers' domains; none could be opened from the build container, and a shared limit of 200
searches cut the research short. Each section names its research file in `docs/research/ideal/`,
where every fact is quoted or marked as paraphrase, with its source. Figures are the documents'
own, in their own years' dollars.

**On the map** ("Show proposals" in the future era, `?proposals=1`): only what a document words

## 1. A second Transbay crossing: which kind, and when

`link21-dumbarton-express-rail.md` §1; `spur-seamless.md` S3, B2.

**Where it stands.** In June 2025 BART's and Capitol Corridor's boards chose a standard-gauge
("Regional Rail") crossing for Link21, and BART decided "not to proceed with a project to design and
construct a second BART transbay rail crossing". Capitol Corridor has run the program since July 1,
2025. No alignment is published: its concepts name places (the Salesforce Transit Center, Alameda,
Jack London Square, West Oakland, the Coliseum, Richmond), and its next planning work waits on a
Caltrans–FRA agreement. No environmental review is scheduled.

| | BART crossing (dropped) | Standard-gauge crossing (chosen) | Upgrade today's corridors first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | Link21 concepts C, D | Link21 concepts A, B, E, F; State Rail Plan 2024; Capitol Corridor's draft vision | Seamless Bay Area (2024) |
| What it serves | Downtown SF, and 4th & Townsend, Mission Bay or San Antonio in some concepts | Through-running at the Salesforce Transit Center, from Sacramento to San José and beyond; high-speed rail | Electrified, frequent megaregional rail on existing routes |
| Riders | 110,000-140,000 new daily rail trips, rising with each SF station served (2024 draft) | "larger travel time saving benefits to a focused number of riders in less established travel markets" | Not quantified |
| Cost | $18-30 billion for the crossing itself, either technology (2023 dollars) | same; Phase 2 alone needs about $800 million of grants, perhaps ten-plus years to secure (BART, 2026) | Less, and sooner: "upgrading existing corridors can occur much more quickly, and at a lower cost" |

**Tradeoffs the documents name**
- **Redundancy against new markets.** SPUR (2016): "The choice comes down to direct redundancy with the
  existing crossing versus leveraging the new crossing to connect new markets." A crossing near the
  existing tube backs it up; one farther south serves Mission Bay, Jack London Square and Alameda.
- **One crossing, or both gauges.** SPUR asked for a four-track tube for both (2008), then for both to
  be studied (2016). Link21 dropped a dual-gauge crossing as "operationally infeasible" and a combined
  BART and regional crossing for "low additional ridership".
- **Now or later.** SPUR (2021) wants the crossing planned now; Seamless Bay Area (2024) asks Link21 to
  "de-prioritize its efforts on the 2nd Transbay Crossing" for corridor upgrades. Plan Bay Area 2050+
  (March 2026) funds only Link21's project development; BART calls a Regional Rail crossing
  unrealistic "within a reasonable timeframe".
- **What carries the load meanwhile.** BART's Core Capacity program aims at 40% more transbay capacity
  through today's tube, with longer and more frequent trains, in under ten years.
- **What it depends on.** Through-running at the Salesforce Transit Center needs The Portal (layer A);
  Capitol Corridor's draft vision calls Richmond to the Coliseum its most complex stretch.

**For your network:** which gauge, where it lands in San Francisco and the East Bay, and whether it
belongs in the network at all against corridor upgrades. Any line you draw for it is yours: no
document gives a path.

## 2. San Francisco's west side: a Geary/19th Avenue subway, or surface rail

`san-francisco.md` §1-3; `spur-seamless.md` S4.

**The subway (SFCTA, 2026; on the map in part).** A two-track subway of about 10 miles with 5-6
stations, from downtown under Geary Blvd, south through Golden Gate Park, under 19th Ave, rising to an
elevated end near the 19th Ave / I-280 interchange and Daly City BART. $20-30 billion (2026 dollars).
Up to 182,000 daily riders on a San Francisco-only line (162,000-182,000 in the table), about 310,000
with East Bay and South Bay connections by BART; 16-19 minutes from Daly City to downtown. The
strategic case found it "both needed and viable" and chose no alignment, stations or technology.

**What's still open, each a choice for you**
- **Technology.** BART allows through-running to SFO and Millbrae but needs longer platforms and costs
  more; standard gauge needs a transfer near Daly City or Colma but could run through to the East Bay;
  Muni Metro can't interoperate with regional services; automated light rail allows smaller stations
  but needs its own yard.
- **Downtown.** Straight under Geary (if a transbay line comes by Mission Bay and 3rd St), or by Howard
  St and Civic Center to the Salesforce Transit Center, a little slower but serving Civic Center.
- **The turn south,** between Masonic (Japantown, the Western Addition, USF, UCSF) and Park Presidio (the
  Richmond).
- **The south end:** through-running or a transfer, at Daly City or Colma (a Colma transfer "may be
  prohibitive").

**Its stated costs and risks:** tunneling and stations "drive the majority of costs"; "Both BART and
Muni Metro subway service would be taken offline for at least 1 – 2 years" (the context, a tie-in
with today's subways, needs checking); a 10-20 acre yard site is "extremely limited"; existing
funding "not enough". SFMTA: "high capital cost, a scarcity of funding sources, and the scale of
disruption during construction". Geary rail met neighborhood opposition in the 1950s and 1980s.

**The surface alternatives**
- **Geary's buses** (built and building): Geary Rapid is complete, and the $48 million Geary Boulevard
  Improvement Project (Stanyan to 34th Ave) is under construction. SFCTA: the subway "is intended to
  complement, not replace" the 38 and 38R.
- **SPUR (2002):** surface light rail on Geary "from the ocean to Laguna", then a subway downtown
  ($1.7 billion then); light rail on 19th Ave along the 28's route; light rail on Van Ness.
- **The M Ocean View under 19th Ave** (SFMTA, 2015-17; $2.5-3 billion) was dropped: ConnectSF chose
  "aggressive surface optimization" for the M instead, and the Muni Metro Capacity Study (December
  2025) plans three-car M and N trains and room for a J Church extension to Stonestown.

**Also tied to Link21:** Link21 found a branch off the Market Street subway infeasible, and the
subway's East Bay ridership depends on Link21's technology and route.

## 3. North Beach and Fisherman's Wharf: the Central Subway extension

`san-francisco.md` §4; `spur-seamless.md` S4. On the map: the Columbus Ave and Powell St options.

SFMTA's 2015 concept study put a North Beach station at Washington Square in every alignment, then
four ways to the Wharf: two-way on Columbus Ave (to Conrad Square), two-way on Powell St (to
Kirkland Yard at Powell and Beach), Powell and Beach Sts, or a one-way loop; subway or surface. $367
million (subway and surface to Kirkland) to $1.4 billion (subway to all three places), in 2014
dollars; it recommended none. SPUR's summary of the city's study: the North Beach and Wharf stops
would lift T line ridership by about half. Since 2026, a new study looks at a one-station extension
into North Beach through the existing tunnels. SPUR (2002) had also floated taking it north from
Chinatown, then on the surface into the Marina by Lombard or Chestnut to a Presidio terminal.

**The choice:** North Beach only, or on to the Wharf; Columbus or Powell; subway or surface.

## 4. Caltrain south of The Portal, and the Peninsula's capacity

`san-francisco.md` §4; `link21-dumbarton-express-rail.md` §3; `spur-seamless.md` S6.

- **Pennsylvania Avenue Extension** (SFCTA, under study): a Caltrain tunnel "along Seventh Street and
  Pennsylvania Avenue", about $2.0-2.5 billion, not before the mid to late 2030s, removing the
  street crossings at Mission Bay Drive and 16th Street; each alternative reworks or moves the 22nd
  Street station. No portals are published, so it isn't on the map.
- **Caltrain's ceiling.** Its Long-Range Service Vision (adopted October 2025) caps the corridor at 8
  Caltrain and 4 high-speed trains per direction at the peak, with short four-track overtakes (around
  Millbrae, Hayward Park/Hillsdale, Redwood City, and California Ave as an option), and drops the 2019
  vision's "Expanded Growth". Any added Peninsula service, from Dumbarton or a second crossing, has to
  fit under it.
- **A Bayview Caltrain station** at Oakdale Ave (SFCTA's recommended site, final report fall 2026).
- **SPUR's Caltrain Corridor Vision Plan** (2017, with partners): frequent all-day service San
  Francisco to San José, $16-21 billion.

## 5. The Dumbarton corridor: rail or a busway

`link21-dumbarton-express-rail.md` §2; `spur-seamless.md` B3. On the map: the 2017 rail shuttle and
the busway.

| | Rail (2017 study; superseded) | Busway (2025-27 study; in Plan Bay Area 2050+) |
|---|---|---|
| What | A shuttle over the Dumbarton rail bridge, Redwood City to Newark, then Union City by 2030, then trains through to Caltrain, ACE and the Capitol Corridor by 2035 | A dedicated busway and trail on the rail corridor, Middlefield Rd (Redwood City) to University Ave (East Palo Alto), nine stations, with express buses Union City to Redwood City |
| Cost | About $975 million to Newark, $295 million more to Union City, $327 million more for 2035 | $606 million with the express buses (Plan Bay Area 2050+) |
| Riders, time | 15,600 transbay riders a day | About 13 minutes end to end |
| When | — | Construction after 2031, funding permitting |

**Tradeoffs the documents name.** SamTrans's 2023 market assessment: "Insufficient ridership
projections to justify cost - estimated at $3.6B in 2024 dollars". The 2017 study found "the bus
alternatives performed the best in terms of cost-effectiveness", while rail had "the greatest
potential for private investment and long-term ridership gains". MTC: rail is "infeasible in the
mid-term due to constraints at Redwood City and capacity along the peninsula". The rail bridge is "a
safety hazard in its current condition", and removing it would cost about $150 million. The busway
"will not preclude" rail later; the rails stay "intact for potential future use". Two other rail
plans came and went: Cross Bay Transit Partners (Facebook and Plenary, about $3 billion, 24,000 trips
a day; ended 2021) and Plan Bay Area 2050's group rapid transit ($3.84 billion; replaced in 2026).
Seamless Bay Area (2026) asks for the Redwood City connection, a link to Union City BART, and evening
and weekend service.

## 6. Regional rail as one network

`link21-dumbarton-express-rail.md` §3; `spur-seamless.md` S1, S2, B1, B2.

No official document names a "regional express rail" network. What there is:
- **California State Rail Plan (2024):** a 2050 zero-emission network with "pulsed" all-day timetables
  and timed transfers, through-running from Sacramento to San José by a second crossing; $307 billion
  statewide.
- **Plan Bay Area 2050+ and Transit 2050+ (adopted March 2026):** "nearly $340 billion" in transit over
  25 years, about 60% more service and 41% more riders by 2050; 15-minute or better service between
  urban centers; Caltrain to 6, then 8, trains an hour each way at the peak.
- **Capitol Corridor's Vision Plan (draft, adoption planned November 2026):** 15-minute Sacramento–
  Oakland trains in the long term, possibly electrified at up to 150 mph; 15 round trips a day to San
  José.
- **SPUR (2019):** a megaregional pulse network on existing tracks, hubs at San Francisco, Oakland, San
  José, Stockton and Sacramento; anywhere in the Bay Area in 60 minutes. **SPUR (2020-21):** a
  regional express bus network on freeways, and a regional transit coordinator.
- **Seamless Bay Area's Vision Map (2019):** a metro of BART, an electrified Caltrain and light rail
  "ringing the Bay", regional express rail, rapid and express buses; modeled at 2.5-7 times the jobs
  reachable by transit. Its lines exist only on its map.

These run on today's lines, so they're questions of service rather than track: how often, how
connected, and under whom. Both groups ask for a regional body to run it: SPUR's regional transit
coordinator, and the governance framework Seamless Bay Area calls for.

## 7. What only you can decide

Nothing here is chosen for you. The questions the proposals leave open:

1. **A second crossing:** standard gauge, BART, both, or none for now; where it lands.
2. **The west side:** a Geary/19th subway (which technology, which downtown route, where it turns),
   surface rail, or better buses.
3. **North Beach and the Wharf:** how far, which street, subway or surface.
4. **Dumbarton:** rail, the busway, or both in turn.
5. **Regional rail:** whether your network shows service (frequencies, pulses) or only track.
6. **Advocacy maps:** whether to trace Seamless Bay Area's or SPUR's maps into the reference layer
   (a license question: their maps are copyrighted).
7. **Anything not here:** your own lines need no proposal behind them; `based_on` can be empty.

## How to draw your picks

1. Open the map with `?edit=1` (the future era opens with the editor in the panel).
2. "Draw a line": click points (they snap to lines on the map; hold Shift not to), and click the last
   point again to finish. Name it, set its mode, tick the proposals it's based on, add notes.
3. "Export network.geojson", and commit it as `data/ideal/network.geojson`; `npm test` checks it.
   A draft stays in your browser until then.

Once the file has lines, `make app-data` puts them on the map: at full strength in the future era,
under "Show Matthew's network", each with a panel naming what it's based on. Say if it should look
otherwise.
